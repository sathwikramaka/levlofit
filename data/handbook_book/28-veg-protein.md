## Chapter overview
Vegetarian and vegan diets can support muscle gain when protein is planned carefully: protein-dense foods at every meal and a mix of sources. Direct evidence in vegan diets is limited.

## Dense sources
Dry soya chunks are the richest common vegetarian source, at about 51.5 g of protein in 100 g. This value is from a close match, defatted soy flour. {usda174275}
| Food, per 100 g raw | Protein | Energy |
| Masoor dal | 24.4 g | 322 kcal |
| Moong dal | 23.9 g | 326 kcal |
| Peanuts | 23.7 g | 520 kcal |
| Chana dal | 21.6 g | 329 kcal |
| Paneer | 18.9 g | 258 kcal |
These values are from the Indian Food Composition Tables. {ifct2017}

## A high-protein vegetarian day
| Meal | Options |
| Breakfast | Moong chilla or besan cheela, or poha with peanuts and curd |
| Lunch | Dal plus rajma or chana with roti; pulses and grains cover each other's gaps |
| Snack | Roasted chana, sprouts chaat or a glass of milk |
| Dinner | Soya chunk curry, paneer bhurji or tofu with vegetables |

## Staples
| Vegetarian | Vegan |
| Paneer, curd and milk | Tofu and soya chunks |
| Dals, chana and rajma | Dals, chana, rajma and sprouts |
| Soya chunks | Peanuts |
> tip: Levlofit rule
> If you eat vegan, aim for the upper end of your protein range. This is a Levlofit rule, not a research finding.

## Soya and hormones
> evidence: Evidence: strong
> Soy did not change male hormones across clinical studies, at any dose tested. {soy2021}

## Key takeaways
- Vegetarian and vegan diets can support muscle gain when protein is planned carefully. Direct evidence in vegan diets is limited.
- Soya chunks, dals, peanuts and paneer are dense sources.
- Mix pulses and grains through the day.

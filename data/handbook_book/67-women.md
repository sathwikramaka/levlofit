## Chapter overview
Women build muscle much as men do: across studies, muscle grew by a similar percentage in women and men. {refalo2025}

## Myths and evidence
| Myth | Evidence |
| You must train differently in each cycle phase | Cycle effects on performance are small and individual |
| Avoid exercise when pregnant | Exercise is safe and helpful in most pregnancies |

## Training with your cycle
> evidence: Evidence: moderate
> A meta-analysis of 78 studies found performance may be slightly lower in the early follicular phase, but effects were trivial and varied, so general phase-based rules are not supported. {mcnulty2020}
- **Track.** Keep your own note of energy and mood, if that helps.
- **Adjust.** On low days lower the load a little; on good days push.
- **Keep going.** No phase needs you to stop training.

## Pregnancy
The Canadian guideline points to 150 minutes a week of moderate activity during a healthy pregnancy. {mottola2018}
> evidence: Evidence: strong
> WHO advises pregnant and postpartum women without contraindications to stay active throughout pregnancy and after birth, with at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity a week and a mix of aerobic and muscle-strengthening work. Women who were active before pregnancy can usually continue. {who2020}
| Pregnant: usually fine | Stop and seek care |
| Get your doctor's go-ahead first | Bleeding, or fluid that keeps leaking |
| Walking, cycling, swimming, light lifting | Dizziness or faintness that does not settle with rest |
| Moderate effort: you can still talk | Chest pain |
| | Painful regular contractions |
| | Breathlessness that does not settle with rest |

## After giving birth
> warn: Ask first
> Levlofit does not yet have an after-birth setting. Ask your doctor or midwife when and how to restart, especially after a caesarean or a difficult birth.

## Key takeaways
- Women build muscle much as men do: similar percentage gains from the same training.
- Menstrual cycle effects on performance are small and individual.
- Pregnancy: usually stay active with your doctor's go-ahead. After birth, restart with your doctor's or midwife's advice.

## Chapter overview
A programme is a set of sessions with rules for when to add weight, reps or sets. Following a proven one beats making it up as you go: it balances the body, spaces out recovery, and tells you exactly what to do next. Many good programmes work; the one you keep doing is the best one. Levlofit builds yours from your goals, days, equipment and level.

## What a good programme gives you
:: compare A proven programme | Making it up each day
Structure that fits your week | Sessions that depend on your mood
Rest between hard sessions for the same muscles | Some muscles hit three days running, others never
A rule for progress, so you keep getting stronger | The same weights for months
Every major muscle covered | Lots of chest and arms, little back and legs
Effort set for your level | Sessions that are too easy or too punishing
::

## How to choose
:: flow Picking a starting point
New to strength training, or back after a long break? | Start with a simple full-body plan, 2 or 3 days a week, for about 3 months. | Go on to the next question.
Can you train 4 or more days a week? | An upper/lower or push/pull/legs split, each muscle twice a week. | Full body, 2 or 3 days a week.
::
The two things that matter most are the days you can really train and the equipment you can really use. After that, effort and consistency decide results far more than the programme's name.

## Full body or split?
:: compare Full body | Split (upper/lower, push/pull/legs)
Each session trains the whole body | Each session trains part of the body
Best with 2 or 3 days a week | Best with 4 to 6 days a week
Each muscle 2 to 3 times a week | Each muscle about twice a week when well designed
Ideal for beginners, who recover fast | Suits more sets per muscle as you advance
::
For muscle growth, training a muscle at least twice a week works better than once, when total sets are equal. {schoenfeld2016f} For strength, frequency matters little once weekly sets are the same, so pick the layout that fits your week. {grgic2018f} A split that hits each muscle only once a week ("bro split") wastes a beginner's fast recovery.

## Well-known programmes, in plain words
These are widely used and known to work. Levlofit does not copy them; it uses the same principles.
| Type | What it looks like | Who it suits |
| Simple linear progression | 3 full-body days a week, alternating two sessions of 5 main lifts; add a little weight every session while you can | Complete beginners, first 3 months |
| Tiered linear progression (the GZCLP style) | Each day: one heavy main lift, one lighter secondary lift, then accessories; when you miss reps, change sets and reps before cutting weight | Beginners after the first months |
| Wave on a training max (the 5/3/1 style) | Work from 90% of an estimated max; three-week waves of 5, 3 and 1 rep sets, then extra lighter sets and accessories; raise the max a little each cycle | Late beginners and up, who want steady long-term progress |
| Push/pull/legs | 6 days: push, pull, legs, twice each, with main lifts and accessories | Those with time and good recovery |
| Dumbbell-only plan | Full body or push/pull/legs with dumbbells and bodyweight | Home trainees without a barbell |
| Bodyweight plan | Push-up, pull-up, dip, squat and core progressions that get harder by lever, not weight | No equipment |

## Beginner linear progression, in numbers
Two sessions, A and B, on three days with a rest day between (A, B, A one week; B, A, B the next). Each lift is 3 sets of 5, with the last set taken to as many reps as you can while stopping 1 to 2 reps before your form breaks.
| Session | Lifts, each 3 x 5 (last set as many as you can) |
| A | Barbell row, bench press, squat |
| B | Chin-up (or lat pulldown), overhead press, deadlift |
:: steps The rules that make it work
Warm up | Start with the empty bar for 5 reps, then add 5 to 10 kg a set while the bar still moves fast.
Rest | 2 to 3 minutes between sets; up to 5 minutes between lifts when you need it.
Add weight | Every session: 1.25 kg on upper-body lifts, 2.5 kg on squat and deadlift. More than 10 reps on the last set: add double.
Reset | Fewer than 15 reps across the 3 sets: take 10% off next time and beat your old rep numbers on the way back up.
Cardio | One easy session (brisk walk, cycle, 15 to 30 minutes) and one hard session (intervals) a week.
::
No 1.25 kg plates? Add 2.5 kg every second session and chase more reps on the second one, or take 2.5 kg steps and accept earlier resets. After about 3 months, most people move to one of the plans below.

## Tiered progression, in numbers
Each day has three tiers: a heavy main lift (tier 1), a second main lift done lighter for more reps (tier 2), and an accessory (tier 3). When you cannot finish all the reps, you keep the weight and move to the next stage, which spreads the same work into more, smaller sets.
| Tier | Stage 1 | Stage 2 | Stage 3 | Rest |
| 1, heavy main lift | 5 x 3, last set as many as you can | 6 x 2, last set as many as you can | 10 x 1, last set as many as you can | 3 to 5 min |
| 2, second main lift | 3 x 10 | 3 x 8 | 3 x 6 | 2 to 3 min |
| 3, accessory | 3 x 15, last set as many as you can | - | - | 1 to 1.5 min |
There are four days, rotating through three training days a week, so day 4 lands on the next Monday.
| Day | Tier 1 | Tier 2 | Tier 3 |
| 1 | Squat | Bench press | Lat pulldown |
| 2 | Overhead press | Deadlift | Dumbbell row |
| 3 | Bench press | Squat | Lat pulldown |
| 4 | Deadlift | Overhead press | Dumbbell row |
:: steps Tiered progression rules
Add weight | After each session: 2.5 kg on bench and overhead press, 5 kg on squat and deadlift (tiers 1 and 2).
Tier 3 | Add weight when the last set reaches 25 reps.
Failed the last stage, tier 1 | Find a new 5-rep max and restart stage 1 at 85% of it.
Failed the last stage, tier 2 | Restart stage 1 at your last stage-1 weight plus 7.5 to 10 kg.
::

## Training-max waves, in numbers
All weights come from a training max: work up to a weight you can lift 3 to 5 times with good speed, estimate your one-rep max from it, and take 90% of that. The training max is kept a little light on purpose, so every session is done well.
| Week | Set 1 | Set 2 | Set 3 (as many as you can) | Then 5 x 5 at |
| Warm-up, every week | 5 at 40% | 5 at 50% | 3 at 60% | - |
| 1 | 5 at 65% | 5 at 75% | 5 or more at 85% | 65% |
| 2 | 3 at 70% | 3 at 80% | 3 or more at 90% | 70% |
| 3 | 5 at 75% | 3 at 85% | 1 or more at 95% | 75% |
A three-day layout: squat and bench press on day 1, deadlift and overhead press on day 2, bench press and squat on day 3. Each day also gets 50 to 100 reps each of one push (dips, push-ups, dumbbell press), one pull (chin-ups, rows, face pulls) and one single-leg or core move (lunges, step-ups, ab work), in any sets you like.
:: steps Training-max rules
Each 3-week cycle | Raise the training max by 2.5 kg on bench and overhead press, 5 kg on squat and deadlift.
Test every 3 cycles | Raise the max as usual, then work up to a set at 100% of it: 3 or more reps means it is right; fewer means re-estimate your max from that set and reset.
Missing reps for a whole cycle | Lower the training max; it has run ahead of you.
No fixed deload week | Older versions rested every fourth week; the test week does that job now.
::
The extra work after the three main sets comes in a few common forms, each using that week's percentages:
| Extra work | Load | Sets and reps |
| Repeat the first set | 65, 70, 75% in weeks 1, 2, 3 | 5 x 5 |
| Repeat the second set | 75, 80, 85% | 5 x 5 |
| Repeat the last set | 85, 90, 90% | 5 x 5 |
| Many light sets | A low percentage, often 50 to 60% | 5 x 10 |
| Many first-set fives | 65, 70, 75% | 10 x 5 |
| One long set | 65, 70, 75% | 1 x 15-20 |
Common variants also keep every main set at 5 reps with no all-out last set, or swap weeks 1 and 2. A weekly version raises the max from one all-out set:
| Reps on the all-out set | Add to the training max |
| 0 or 1 | Nothing |
| 2 or 3 | 2.5 kg |
| 4 or 5 | 2.5 to 5 kg |
| More than 5 | 5 to 7.5 kg |

## Six-day push/pull/legs, in numbers
Push, pull, legs, then again, with a rest day either in the middle or at the end of the week. The first lift each day is heavy and progresses every session; the rest are 8 to 12 or 15 to 20 reps for muscle.
| Day | Main lift | Then |
| Pull | Deadlift 1 x 5 or more, alternating sessions with barbell row 4 x 5 and 1 x 5 or more | Pulldown or pull-up 3 x 8-12; seated or chest-supported row 3 x 8-12; face pull 5 x 15-20; hammer curl 4 x 8-12; dumbbell curl 4 x 8-12 |
| Push | Bench press 4 x 5 and 1 x 5 or more, alternating sessions with overhead press | The other press 3 x 8-12; incline dumbbell press 3 x 8-12; pushdown 3 x 8-12 paired with lateral raise 3 x 15-20; overhead triceps extension 3 x 8-12 paired with lateral raise 3 x 15-20 |
| Legs | Squat 2 x 5 and 1 x 5 or more | Romanian deadlift 3 x 8-12; leg press 3 x 8-12; leg curl 3 x 8-12; calf raise 5 x 8-12 |
Add 2.5 kg a session on the presses, rows and squat, and 5 kg on the deadlift. For the 8 to 12 moves, add weight once every set reaches 12.

## Dumbbell-only start, in numbers
For training at home with adjustable dumbbells, until you can reach a barbell. Two workouts, three days a week (A, B, A, then B, A, B).
| Workout | Moves |
| A | Split squat or lunge; dumbbell floor press; straight-leg deadlift; plank |
| B | Split squat or lunge; seated shoulder press (on a bench or on the floor); two-dumbbell bent-over row; plank |
Do 3 sets of as many reps as you can, up to 10, resting 1 minute. When all 3 sets reach 10, go heavier. Hold the plank as long as you can, 3 times. Add dips and pull-ups the same way if you can. The plan runs out when your dumbbells stop going heavier; then move to a barbell plan above.

## Progress in more than one way
:: steps Ways a programme moves you forward
More weight | The classic: add the smallest step when every set reaches the top of the rep range.
More reps | Same weight, one or two more reps. Counts as real progress.
More sets | An extra set for a muscle that is lagging, when recovery allows.
Better technique | Deeper, slower, steadier reps with the same weight.
::
Expect stalls. When one lift stalls for two or three sessions, take 10% off and build back up with more reps. See [[progressive-overload|Progressive overload]] and [[plateaus|Breaking through plateaus]].

## Rep ranges
:: scale What different rep ranges are best at | {schoenfeld2017l}
1 to 5 | Maximal strength | Heaviest loads; most skill practice for heavy lifts; long rests.
6 to 12 | Muscle and strength together | The most time-efficient range for most people.
12 to 20 | Muscle, endurance | Builds as much muscle as heavier sets when taken close to failure; burns more.
20 to 30 or more | Muscle, endurance | Works too, but sets are long and uncomfortable.
::
Light and heavy sets build similar muscle when each set ends close to failure; heavy sets build more maximal strength. {schoenfeld2017l} No rep range "tones" or "burns fat": fat loss comes from eating less. Most good programmes mix ranges.

## Should you train to failure?
:: band How close to failure to stop | reps left in reserve | {refalo2023}
0 | 1 | Failure: fine on safe isolation moves now and then | ok
1 | 3 | Where most sets should end: close enough to grow | good
3 | 5 | Good for heavy compound lifts while learning technique | good
5 | 8 | Too easy to drive much growth | bad
::
Sets stopped 1 to 3 reps short of failure build about as much muscle as sets to failure, with less fatigue. {refalo2023} People misjudge how close they are, so take a set to failure now and then on a safe move to calibrate. Beginners should not grind squats, deadlifts or presses to failure while learning them.

## Checking a programme someone gave you
Ask these questions about any plan, from a website, a friend or a trainer:
- Does it train every major muscle group about twice a week?
- Does it have a clear rule for adding weight, reps or sets?
- Are the weekly sets reasonable: roughly 10 to 20 hard sets per muscle for most people?
- Does it fit the days and time you really have?
- Does it include some cardio and rest days?
If most answers are yes, follow it for at least 3 months before judging it. If it is a long list of random exercises with no progression rule, choose another.

## When to change programmes
Stay on one programme for at least 3 to 6 months. Change when you have stalled for weeks despite good sleep and food, when your goal changes, or when your schedule does. Changing every few weeks resets your progress tracking and is the most common reason people spin their wheels.

## Key takeaways
- Follow a proven plan; the best one is the one you will keep doing.
- Beginners do best on full body, 2 or 3 days a week; each muscle at least twice a week.
- Any rep range builds muscle when sets end 1 to 3 reps short of failure.
- Progress by weight, reps, sets or better technique, and stay with a plan for months.

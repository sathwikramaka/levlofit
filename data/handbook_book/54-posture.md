title: Posture and movement
summary: Where the evidence disagrees with the usual story.

> warn: Read this first
> The familiar story says certain postures are "poor", weak backs and tight chests cause them, and exercises correct them. The evidence does not support that story as stated. Posture is associated with some outcomes, and has not been shown to cause them. Most of the practical advice stays. What changes is why: comfort, strength and appearance, not correction.

## Posture and back pain
> evidence: Evidence: no consensus on cause
> A review of 41 systematic reviews looked at spine posture, physical activity and low back pain. They reported both positive and null associations for spine posture, prolonged standing, sitting, bending and twisting, awkward postures and heavy physical work. {swain2020}
> Its conclusion: "Despite the availability of many reviews, there is no consensus regarding causality of physical exposure to LBP. Association has been documented but does not provide a causal explanation for LBP." {swain2020}
This does not say posture never matters, and it is no recommendation to slouch. It says the confident story, where bad posture causes your pain and fixing it cures your pain, is not established.

## Forward head posture and neck pain
> evidence: Evidence: association in adults, not established as cause
> A review of 15 cross-sectional studies found adults with neck pain had more forward head posture than those without. In adolescents there was no significant difference. Age played an important role. {mahmoud2019}
> All the studies were cross-sectional, which means they measured people at one point in time, so this design cannot show which came first.
The missing link in adolescents is interesting. If this posture simply caused neck pain, you might expect the link at all ages. It might instead reflect age, or people in pain holding themselves differently. That is reasoning, not a finding.

## What this means for the advice
| Usual framing | What this Handbook says |
| "Signs of poor posture" | Postural variations. They are descriptions, not diagnoses. |
| "A weak upper back causes rounded shoulders" | Not established. This is a mechanical story. |
| "Tight chest muscles pull your shoulders forward" | Not established. Stretching does not lengthen muscle anyway. {ingram2025} |
| "Correct your posture with these exercises" | The exercises are worth doing. "Correct" claims too much. |
| "Better alignment means safer lifting" | Not established here. Technique is a sensible default. |
| "Good posture improves your appearance" | Fair, as a claim about appearance. |

## The advice that stays, and why
| Practice | Honest reason |
| Train your upper back | It is a major muscle group that responds to training. |
| Move regularly during long sitting | Reasonable and low-cost, for comfort and activity. |
| Use a workstation you find comfortable | Comfort is reason enough. |
| Stretch your chest and front shoulders, if you like | It can improve range over months. {ingram2025} Loaded full-range training also does this. {alizadeh2023} |
| Train your trunk and hips | Ordinary training reasons. |
| Change position through the day | Since no single correct posture is established here, moving around is a reasonable default. |
> tip: The short version
> There is no one posture you must achieve. Move often, and train your upper back for the same reasons you train everything else.

## Pain is a separate question
> warn: Get it checked
> Nothing in this chapter is advice about pain. If your neck or back pain lasts, see a qualified clinician instead of starting a correction programme.
> Get checked promptly if pain spreads into an arm or leg, or comes with numbness, tingling or weakness.
> Get checked promptly if pain follows a fall, collision or other injury.
> Get checked promptly if pain wakes you at night, or no position relieves it.
> Get checked promptly if pain comes with fever, unexplained weight loss or a history of cancer.
> **Any change in bladder or bowel control, or numbness around the groin or inner thighs, is an emergency.**

## Common mistakes
- **Treating a postural variation as an injury waiting to happen.** That cause is not established.
- **Buying a correction programme sold on that claim.**
- **Reading the adult link as proof of cause.** The studies cannot show it.
- **Holding one "correct" position for hours.** Move instead.
- **Stretching to lengthen a muscle.** It does not do that.
- **Using posture work instead of getting pain checked.**

## Key takeaways
- There is no consensus that posture or physical exposure causes low back pain.
- Forward head posture is linked with neck pain in adults, not in adolescents, from one-time-point studies only.
- Train your upper back for training reasons.
- Move often. No single position is established as correct.
- Lasting pain needs a clinician.

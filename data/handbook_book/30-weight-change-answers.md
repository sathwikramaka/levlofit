## Chapter overview
Weight change follows one rule: eat less energy than you use and you lose weight; eat more and you gain. Every diet that works does it by changing that balance. {hall2017} The hard part is that both sides of the balance are only estimates, so the scale over weeks is your real measure.

## The two estimates that go wrong
:: compare Why people do not lose | Why people do not gain
They eat more than they think: oil, drinks, snacks, sauces and weekends add up | They eat less than they think: small breakfasts, skipped meals, busy days
Their energy use is lower than the calculator said | Their energy use is higher than the calculator said
Calories "burned" by a watch were eaten back | Extra activity was not covered with extra food
::
In a classic study, people who said they could not lose weight on very little food were eating about twice what they reported and moving less than they reported. {lichtman1992} This is normal human error, not dishonesty. The fix is the same both ways: track honestly for two to three weeks and adjust by the trend.

## Setting your calories
:: steps Finding the number that works for you
Start with an estimate | Your Levlofit plan already gives one; any calculator is only a starting point.
Adjust by goal | About 10 to 20% below maintenance to lose, 10 to 20% above to gain.
Weigh in regularly | Same time, same conditions: morning, after the toilet, before food.
Read the trend | Compare weekly averages, not single days. Daily weight can swing by 1 to 2 kg from water, salt, food and glycogen.
Adjust after 2 to 3 weeks | No change in the trend: move calories 100 to 200 a day in the direction you need.
Repeat as you change | Your energy use falls as you lose weight and rises as you gain, so targets need updating.
::
See [[calories-body-composition|Calories and body composition]] and [[body-weight|Body weight tracking]].

## Bulk or cut?
:: flow Which comes first
Clearly carrying extra fat? | Lose weight first, and lift to keep your muscle. | Go on to the next question.
Clearly thin? | Gain weight with lifting; some fat gain is expected and fine. | Go on to the next question.
Normal weight but soft and not strong ("skinny fat")? | Build muscle at a small surplus, or at maintenance as a beginner; choose the phase you mind less. | Lean enough but not big enough: gain. Big enough but not lean enough: lose.
::
If your goal is performance, avoid hard cuts. Neither choice is permanent: you can gain then lose, or lose then gain. Long-term body change asks you to accept a few months of looking a little fatter or a little smaller than you would like.

## Losing fat and gaining muscle at once
It happens most in beginners, people returning after a break, and people carrying more fat. {barakat2020} Lean, experienced lifters rarely manage both. Losing slowly helps: athletes who lost about 0.7% of body weight a week gained muscle, while those losing about 1.4% a week did not. {garthe2011}
:: band Weekly weight loss and keeping muscle | % of body weight a week | {garthe2011}
0 | 0.5 | Slow: easiest to keep or even gain muscle | good
0.5 | 1 | Steady: the usual target; keep lifting and eat enough protein | good
1 | 1.5 | Fast: more muscle at risk; fine for short phases with extra fat to lose | ok
1.5 | 3 | Very fast: muscle loss, hunger and rebound more likely | bad
::
Recomposition is slower and harder to track, because the scale may not move. If the scale is not moving and you are not sure it is working, pick one direction for a few months.

## Metabolism, starvation mode and body types
:: compare Common belief | What the research shows
My metabolism is unusually slow or fast | Most of the difference in resting energy use between people is explained by body size and muscle; true outliers are rare and medical. {johnstone2005}
Eating too little puts me in starvation mode and stops weight loss | Energy use falls somewhat as you lose weight, but a moderate deficit still works; weight loss does not stop while you truly eat less.
Ectomorphs, mesomorphs and endomorphs need different diets | Body types come from a 1940s theory about personality; they do not predict how you gain or lose. Everyone follows the same energy balance.
I am a hard gainer | Hard gainers who eat more, gain. Some people need far more food than they expect.
::
If you truly eat less over months and the scale does not move, or eat much more and still do not gain, see a doctor: a thyroid or other medical problem is then possible.

## Body-fat scales and gadgets
Home body-fat scales and handheld devices use electrical impedance, which shifts with water, food and time of day; readings are often off by several percent and not even consistently off. {ward2019} Use body weight, a waist measurement and photos instead. If your weight is falling, you are losing fat. See [[body-measurements|Body measurements]] and [[progress-photos|Progress photos]].

## Losing weight: lift too
Lifting while you lose weight keeps your muscle, so more of what you lose is fat and you look better at the end. Hunger is normal in a deficit; protein, vegetables, fibre and enough sleep help. Plan a few weeks at maintenance after 8 to 12 weeks of losing, to rest from dieting. See [[body-fat-lean|Body fat and getting lean]].

## Gaining weight: eat more than you think
If the scale is not going up, you are not eating enough yet, whatever the plan says. Add 200 to 300 kcal a day and check again in two weeks. Easy extras: milk, curd, peanuts and peanut butter, bananas, an extra roti, ghee on dal, a shake. See [[muscle-gain|Gaining muscle and weight]].

## Macros or calories?
For weight, calories come first; diets with very different carbs and fat lose the same weight when calories and protein are matched. {hall2017} For body composition, protein comes next; carbs and fat then fill the rest of your calories in whatever mix you enjoy. Keep at least some fat (about 0.6 g per kg body weight is a common floor) for health.

## Key takeaways
- Calories decide weight; both sides of the balance are estimates, so trust your weekly average weight.
- Change calories by 10 to 20% for your goal and adjust every 2 to 3 weeks.
- Bulk or cut by what you mind less; aim to lose about 0.5 to 1% of body weight a week.
- Slow metabolism, starvation mode and body types explain very little; consistent eating explains most.
- Ignore body-fat scales; use weight, waist and photos.

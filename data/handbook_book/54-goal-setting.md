## Chapter overview
Goal setting is taught with great confidence and studied much less. This chapter says which advice has evidence behind it.

## SMART goals are a drafting aid
SMART means specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound. No evidence reviewed here shows that writing a training goal this way changes your results. It is a useful checklist for writing clearly, nothing more. Being measurable and achievable do matter, because this volume can tell you what you can measure and how fast change can happen.

## What has evidence
> evidence: Evidence: moderate
> In ten studies of habit programmes for physical activity, problem solving was linked with better results (0.36, 0.17 to 0.55), and action planning was among the common techniques. The overall effect was small to medium (0.31, 0.14 to 0.48). {ma2023}
> evidence: Evidence: moderate, from two more sources
> **Whose goal it is matters.** A review of 66 studies found consistent support for a link between more autonomous motivation and exercise. {teixeira2012}
> **Choosing your own helps.** In a review of 20 studies, self-selected habits generally gave stronger habits. {smith2024}
> tip: What this recommends
> Write down what you will do and when. Write down what usually stops you and what you will do instead. Choose the goal yourself.

## Process goals and outcome goals
| Type | Status |
| Process goals: the actions you take | The closest fit to the evidence. They are also the part you control. |
| Outcome goals: the result you want | Fine as direction, but limited by biology. |
> warn: Some goals cannot honestly have a date
> "Gain this much muscle in this many weeks" relies on a rate no one can reliably give you. Set an action goal and a strength goal instead.

## Timelines this Handbook can support
| Goal | What you can expect |
| Fat loss | About 0.5 to 1% of body weight a week, slower as you get leaner. {helms2014} This is a range, not a date. |
| Muscle gain | No reliable rate. |
| Muscle gain and fat loss together | Can happen, with no reliable rate. |
| Strength on one lift | Volume helps, but your personal rate is not established. {pelland2025} Judge by direction. |
| A habit feeling automatic | Median 59 to 66 days, ranging from 4 to 335. {smith2024} |
| Visible physique change | Usually years. |

## Writing one down
| Part | Why |
| The action, and when | Action planning. {ma2023} |
| What usually stops you, and what you will do instead | Problem solving. {ma2023} |
| Your own reason | Ideally one that lasts beyond the outcome. {teixeira2012} |
| How you will know | Which tool, how often, and what size of change is bigger than its error. |
> tip: Do not set a goal finer than your tool can measure
> A 1 cm waist target is smaller than the error between duplicate tape measurements can be. {barrios2016} That goal is close to a coin flip you will read as failure.

## Planning around life
A year contains illness, travel, deadlines and obligations. Stress predicted less activity in most prospective studies. {stults2014} Missing one opportunity did not materially affect habit formation. {lally2010}
So decide now what your reduced week looks like. Then a bad week is a choice between your full plan and a smaller one, not your full plan and nothing.

## Common mistakes
- **Treating SMART as a technique.**
- **Putting a date on muscle gain.**
- **Adopting someone else's goal.**
- **Building everything on appearance.** It is a mixed predictor of continuing. {teixeira2012}
- **Setting a target finer than your tool's error.**
- **Writing the action but not the obstacle.**
- **Planning a year with no interruptions.**

## Key takeaways
- SMART helps you write clearly. It is not a tested method.
- Planning your actions and solving problems in advance have the better support.
- The goal should be yours.
- Fat loss is the one result here with a pace you can plan around.
- Define your measure and its error when you set your goal.
- Plan your reduced week before you need it.

## Chapter overview
Cardio guidelines count minutes of moderate or vigorous activity. You can judge both without a heart-rate monitor by rating your effort from 0 to 10, relative to your own fitness.

## Effort out of 10
| Effort | Name | What it counts toward |
| 2 to 4 | Light | Everyday movement such as slow walking. |
| 5 to 6 | Moderate | 150 to 300 minutes a week. |
| 7 to 8 | Vigorous | 75 to 150 minutes a week. |
> evidence: Evidence: strong
> WHO rates light activity at 2 to 4, moderate at 5 or 6 and vigorous at 7 or 8 on a 0 to 10 scale of perceived exertion, relative to each person's capacity. {who2020}

## Three ways to meet the weekly minimum
One vigorous minute counts as two moderate ones.
| Your week | Minutes |
| All moderate | 150 |
| All vigorous | 75 |
| Mixed: 90 moderate plus 30 vigorous | 120 |

### Worked example: a mixed week
- **Moderate.** Brisk walk, 30 minutes on Monday, Wednesday and Friday: 90 moderate minutes.
- **Vigorous.** Jog, 15 minutes on Tuesday and Saturday: 30 vigorous minutes.
- **Add up.** 90 + 2 x 30 = 150. The minimum is met.
Running uses a talk test for the same idea; see [[running-guide|Running]]. Lifting uses a different scale, reps in reserve; see [[exercise-science|Exercise science]].

## Key takeaways
- Rate cardio effort 0 to 10: 5 or 6 is moderate, 7 or 8 is vigorous.
- One vigorous minute counts like two moderate minutes toward the weekly target.
- Mix both to reach at least 150 moderate-equivalent minutes a week.

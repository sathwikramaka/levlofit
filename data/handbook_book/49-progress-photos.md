## Chapter overview
A photo is one of the noisiest tools in this volume, and the method below exists to reduce that noise. Used well, with fixed conditions and long gaps, it shows the thing you care about. Used casually, it shows the lighting.
This chapter is practical guidance. No study here sets the best method or interval.

## What changes between photos
| What varies | Effect |
| Light direction and hardness | Often the largest. Overhead or side light adds definition, and flat front light removes it. |
| Pose, stance and tension | Changes your outline. Flexed against relaxed is not a fair comparison. |
| Camera distance, height and lens | Changes how your proportions look. |
| Time of day, food, fluid, salt and pump | The same daily changes that move your weight, made visible. |
| Tan, hair and clothing | Changes how defined you look, with no real change. |
> warn: Why before-and-after marketing works
> All of these can be changed on purpose in an afternoon. If a transformation photo changes more than one, it is not evidence of anything.

## Fix the conditions
| Fix | Error it removes |
| Same room, light and time of day | Lighting, and some daily variation |
| Camera on a fixed surface at a marked height and distance | Perspective |
| Same stance, with feet on the same marks | Pose |
| Relaxed, every time | Tension. Relaxed is easier to repeat than flexed. |
| Morning, before eating | Food and fluid |
| Not straight after training | Pump |
| Front, both sides and back, every time | Choosing flattering angles |
If you enjoy flexed poses, keep them separately, and compare relaxed with relaxed.

## How often to take them, and how often to look
| Action | Interval | Why |
| Take | Every four weeks | Cheap, and you can never go back and take last year's. |
| Compare | Every three to six months | A month's change is often smaller than the photo's error. |
> tip: Keep every photo
> Keep the ones you dislike too. A comparison needs a starting point, and you can never retake it. Levlofit stores your progress photos privately.

## What photos are not for
| Use | Position |
| Deciding if your plan is working | No. Your training log answers sooner, with less error. |
| Finding a weak muscle | Weak for this. Use your training history. |
| Estimating body fat by eye | No. Even tools built for this do it poorly. {merrigan2022} |
| Motivation | Be careful. Appearance is a mixed predictor of continuing to exercise. {teixeira2012} |
| Comparing yourself with other people's photos | No. Their lighting, pose and skeleton differ. |

## When to stop taking them
> warn: Stop and see a clinician if
> You take or check photos far more often than this chapter suggests, and cannot comfortably stop.
> A photo decides your mood, or whether you eat or train.
> You avoid photos, mirrors or changing rooms because of how you think you look. {cinaroglu2025}
> You stay unhappy with images others describe as unchanged or improved.
> Photos are optional. Your training log is enough by itself.

## Common mistakes
- **Different lighting between photos.** This is often the biggest error, and the easiest to fix.
- **Flexing in one photo and not the other.**
- **Comparing consecutive months.** The change is often smaller than the error.
- **Taking photos straight after training.**
- **Deleting early photos.** You lose your starting point.
- **Using photos to judge your plan.** Use your log.
- **Comparing yourself with photos taken in conditions you cannot see.**

## Key takeaways
- Photos have large error. Fixed conditions are the method.
- Take them monthly. Compare every few months at the earliest.
- Relaxed, same marks, same light, before eating and not after training.
- Your log shows whether it is working. A photo shows how it looks.
- If taking photos has become checking, stop.

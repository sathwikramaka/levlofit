## Chapter overview
Fitness looks complicated because there is so much advice. Results come from a short list of basics, done for a long time. Get these right before you worry about anything else.

:: stats
5 | things | decide almost all of your results | -
150 | min a week | of moderate activity, the health minimum | {who2020}
2 | days a week | of strength training for every adult | {who2020}
::

## The five things that matter most
:: steps What decides your results
Train hard enough | Sets that end a few reps short of failure, on a plan that suits your level.
Progress over time | Add reps, weight or sets as you get stronger, so the work keeps asking more of you.
Eat for your goal | The right calories for losing, keeping or gaining weight, and enough protein every day.
Recover | Sleep, rest days and lighter weeks: the body adapts between sessions, not during them.
Keep going | Months and years of the four above beat any perfect plan you stop after three weeks.
::
Everything else (exact exercises, meal timing, supplements, gadgets) changes results far less. Spending your energy on details while the basics slip is the most common way to stall. See [[progressive-overload|Progressive overload]] and [[nutrition-fundamentals|Nutrition fundamentals]].

## Exercise or training?
:: compare Exercise | Training
For today: you feel good, you move, you burn energy. | For a goal months away: a stronger lift, a faster run, a different body.
Any activity on any day counts. | Each session builds on the last, so the plan matters.
Great for health and mood. | Needs a proven programme, and progress you can measure.
::
Both are good. Levlofit builds a training plan, because that is how bodies change on purpose. If you only want to be active, any movement you enjoy, done most days, meets the health goal. See [[general-fitness|General fitness and heart health]].

## Start smaller than you think
People who fail often try to change everything in one week. Pick one or two changes you are sure you can keep, and add more only when those feel easy.
:: steps A first month that sticks
Week 1 | Train on two fixed days. Keep sessions short. Add one glass of water each morning.
Week 2 | Same days. Add vegetables to one meal a day.
Week 3 | Add a third session if the first two felt easy. Start logging your sets.
Week 4 | Hit your protein target on most days. Weigh in once a week, same time of day.
::
Going to the gym once a week, every week, beats a perfect plan you drop. Habits grow from repetition, not motivation. See [[sustainable-habits|Building habits that last]].

## How fast change really comes
:: bars Realistic first-year changes | per month | -
Muscle, men new to training | 0.9 | about 0.5 to 1 kg a month in the first year, then slower
Muscle, women new to training | 0.45 | about half of that
Fat loss, sustainable pace | 2.5 | 0.5 to 1% of body weight a week (about 2 to 3 kg a month for a 70 kg person)
::
These are rough planning figures from coaching experience, not promises; genetics, age, sleep and how well you eat all move them. Visible change usually needs 2 to 3 months. Muscle comes slower than most people expect; fat loss comes faster than muscle gain. Losing fat and gaining muscle at the same time is possible for beginners and people returning after a break, but slower than doing one at a time. See [[weight-change-answers|Losing and gaining weight: your questions]].

## Every goal is made of the same parts
Whatever you want, it breaks down into some mix of these:
- Lose some fat.
- Gain some muscle.
- Strength training.
- Cardio and conditioning.
- Skill in particular movements (a pull-up, a run, a punch).
- The eating that supports the above.
A "toned" look, for example, is some muscle plus low enough body fat to see it. A marathon is mostly cardio, some strength and a lot of skill practice. Name the mix and the plan becomes obvious.

## Why strength training is worth it for everyone
:: compare Strength training gives you | It does not
Stronger bones and a lower risk of osteoporosis | Make you bulky by accident: building lots of muscle takes years of effort
Better balance and fewer falls as you age | Make you slow or inflexible when you train through a full range
Lower blood pressure and better blood sugar | Need a gym: bands, bodyweight and dumbbells work
Easier daily life: lifting, carrying, climbing stairs | Replace cardio; you want both
::
Cardio adds a stronger heart and lungs, better recovery between sets, mood and sleep. It does not "kill your gains". See [[heart-rate-calories|Heart rate, sweat and calories burned]].

## Motivation is not the plan
Motivation comes and goes. Habits and a plan carry you on the days it does not show up.
- **Love the result, not every session.** Many people never enjoy every workout; they enjoy being fitter.
- **Separate training and play.** Train to your plan, and keep a sport or walk you do just for fun.
- **Keep goals quiet until you have done the work.** Announcing a big goal can feel like progress already made, which makes people less likely to follow through. Share what you have done, not what you will do. {gollwitzer2009}
- **Expect bad weeks.** Miss a day, return the next. One missed week changes nothing; quitting changes everything.

## Key takeaways
- Training hard enough, progressing, eating for your goal, recovering and staying consistent decide almost everything.
- Start with one or two changes you can keep, then build.
- Muscle comes slowly; give any plan 2 to 3 months before judging it.
- Strength training helps everyone, and cardio helps your lifting.

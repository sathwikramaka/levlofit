## Chapter overview
Habit formation has been measured, and the findings are less tidy than the popular version and more useful. It takes longer than you may have heard, people differ enormously, and missing a day matters less than the all-or-nothing story claims.

## How long it takes
> evidence: Evidence: moderate, with results that vary a lot
> A review of 20 studies with 2,601 people looked at health habits. Four studies reported timing: median times of 59 to 66 days, mean times of 106 to 154 days, and individuals ranging from 4 to 335 days. {smith2024}
> Habit scores improved overall (effect size 0.69, 0.49 to 0.88): physical activity 0.69, diet 0.57, flossing 1.11. {smith2024}
> Most studies had a high risk of bias, and the authors note the number of studies was small. {smith2024}
You may have heard "21 days". These results point to much longer. Two months of deliberate repetition before training feels automatic is a normal result. It does not mean you are bad at this.

## What seems to help
These findings come from the same review, on a thin base.
| Factor | Finding |
| Time of day | Morning practice generally gave stronger habits. {smith2024} |
| Who chooses | Self-selected habits generally gave stronger habits. {smith2024} |
> tip: Pick your own habit
> Choose the habit you would actually keep. Attach it to something already steady in your day, and do it in the morning if that works for you. Give it a couple of months before adding another.

## Do habit programmes work?
> evidence: Evidence: moderate, with a small effect
> Ten studies found habit formation programmes increased physical activity habit strength (effect size 0.31, 0.14 to 0.48). Problem solving was linked with better results (0.36, 0.17 to 0.55). Social reward was linked with worse results after adjustment, based on only two studies. {ma2023}
Deliberate habit work helps, by a modest amount. Be sceptical of anyone promising a system that transforms your consistency.
Problem solving is simple: work out what usually stops you, and decide in advance what you will do when it happens.

## Missing a day is not a relapse
In one well-known study, missing one opportunity did not materially affect habit formation. {lally2010} One missed session is a missed session. Most of the damage comes from what you decide next.
| What happened | Reasonable response |
| Missed one session | Train at your next planned time. Nothing else changes. |
| Ate a meal outside your plan | Make your next meal normal. |
| Missed a week for exams, work or illness | Return at a manageable load. Do not try to make up the sessions. |
| Missed a month or more | Treat it as a restart, not a failure. |
"Never miss twice" is a catchy saying, not a finding.

## Habits to consider first
This is a menu, not a schedule, since self-selected habits did better. {smith2024} The order is not based on a direct comparison.
| Habit | Why it earns a place |
| Attending your planned sessions | Your training depends on it. |
| A steady sleep window | Aim for 7 or more hours, regularly. {aasm2015} |
| Reaching your protein target | It has a clear number. See [[protein|Protein]]. |
| Eating in a way you could keep up for a year | Long-term success depends on sticking with it. {aragon2017} |
| Daily walking | Simple, easy to cue and a reasonable first habit. |

## Tracking habits
Self-monitoring was among the methods used in these programmes, though its own effect could not be isolated. {ma2023} A checklist is reasonable, but unproven by itself. Track a few things you chose, and review them weekly. Your Levlofit habit list is enough for this.

## Common mistakes
- **Expecting automatic behaviour in three weeks.** It often takes about two months or longer.
- **Following someone else's habit plan.** Self-selected habits did better.
- **Starting several habits at once.** You may conclude you lack discipline when none stick.
- **Treating one missed day as failure.**
- **Expecting habits to replace training, sleep and protein.** They support those things.
- **Judging a training habit by how it feels.** Look at your attendance over weeks.

## Key takeaways
- Median time to form a habit was about 59 to 66 days, with individuals from 4 to 335 days.
- Habit programmes work, by a modest amount.
- Choose your own habit, attach it to a steady cue, and give it about two months.
- Plan for what usually stops you.
- One missed session does not undo the process.

# Tracking
slug: why-track
title: Why track your progress
summary: What tracking does, and what it does not.

## Chapter overview
Tracking does not build muscle or remove fat. It reduces the error in your own judgement about whether something changed. The tools you use have errors of their own, and much of this volume is about handling them.

## Measuring alone is not enough
"You cannot improve what you never measure" is a management slogan. The closest evidence here suggests something more careful.
> evidence: Evidence: moderate
> A review of randomised trials looked at self-weighing for weight loss. Only one study tested self-weighing alone, and there was no evidence it worked (-0.5 kg, -1.3 to 0.3). {madigan2015}
> Adding self-weighing to a behaviour programme helped (-1.7 kg, -2.6 to -0.8). Programmes that included it beat minimal support (-3.4 kg, -4.2 to -2.6). {madigan2015}
So tracking does not do the work. It helps you steer the training and eating you are already doing.

## What tracking is for
| Purpose | Status |
| Seeing change too slow to notice | The core reason. Muscle and fat change slowly. |
| Deciding whether to change something | A measurement earns its place if some result would change what you do. |
| Accountability | Programmes with accountability lost more weight (-3.6 kg against -2.3 kg, p = 0.03). {madigan2015} |
| Motivating you | Not established. Numbers you feel you must satisfy may not help. {teixeira2012} |
| Proving your plan works | Too much to ask. It can show whether you are progressing. |

## Every measurement has error
A reading is the real value plus error. That error comes from three places.
| Source | What it looks like | What to do |
| The tool | It disagrees with reality. Body-fat scanners agreed poorly with DEXA on 12-week change. {merrigan2022} | Choose tools with less error. |
| Your technique | You do it a little differently each time. | Fix the method: same conditions, same spot, same person. |
| Your body | Water, food, salt, the menstrual cycle and time of day all move it. | Average repeated readings, such as a seven-day weight average. |

## When a number means nothing
React to a change only when it is larger than the error of what measured it. Many weekly changes are not.
| Measure | Main problem | Sensible interval |
| Body weight | Daily swings | Weekly, using a seven-day average |
| Tape measurements | Technique | Monthly, by the same person |
| Photos | Lighting, pose and pump | Take monthly, compare every few months |
| Logged loads and reps | Least error, since these are counts | Every session |
| Body-fat scales | Tracks change poorly | Not recommended for tracking. {merrigan2022} |
> tip: Your training log is your best tool
> Repetitions at a given load are a count. That is why this Handbook keeps sending you back to your log, not to a scale or a mirror.

## Track few things
More numbers do not give a clearer picture. They give noise more chances to look like signal. A sensible minimum:
| What to track | Why |
| Training log | Lowest error, and it records your training directly. |
| Seven-day average weight | Cheap, and averaging handles most of the noise. |
| Waist | Cheap, and more useful than a body-fat reading if your method is steady. |
| Photos at fixed intervals | The slow record. |
Add sleep, food or mood tracking only if you have a specific question.

## When tracking becomes the problem
Three trials in the self-weighing review measured psychological harm, and the two described here found no significant differences between groups. Most trials did not report on adverse effects. {madigan2015} That is not a clean bill of health, and it says nothing about you personally.
> warn: Stop tracking and see a clinician if
> A reading decides your mood for the day, or whether you eat.
> You weigh, measure or photograph yourself more than this volume suggests, and cannot comfortably stop.
> You keep checking the mirror or a body part, or avoid mirrors, changing rooms and photos. {cinaroglu2025}
> Food rules keep narrowing, or eating brings guilt or compensation.
> You cannot stop cutting even after reaching your target.
> Tracking should serve a decision. Nothing in this volume requires you to weigh yourself. Your training log alone is enough.

## Common mistakes
- **Believing measuring causes progress.** Self-weighing alone showed no evidence of working.
- **Reacting to a change smaller than the error.**
- **Tracking a body-fat percentage that cannot track change.**
- **Tracking eight things and calling the noisiest one a trend.**
- **Changing your plan on one week's data.**
- **Continuing to track when it no longer serves a decision.**

## Key takeaways
- Tracking helps steer a plan. It is not the plan.
- Every measurement has error from the tool, your technique and your body.
- React only to changes larger than that error.
- Four measures are plenty. Your training log is the best of them.
- If tracking stops serving a decision, stop.

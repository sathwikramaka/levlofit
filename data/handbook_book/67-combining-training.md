title: Combining different kinds of training
summary: How strength and cardio fit together.

## Chapter overview
Few people train only one thing forever. A runner may want strength, and a lifter may want better fitness. The old fear was that mixing strength and cardio would blunt the results of both. The current evidence is more specific, and kinder.

## What it actually costs
> evidence: Evidence: strong
> A review of 43 studies compared combined aerobic and strength training with strength training alone. It found no meaningful loss in maximal strength (effect size -0.06, -0.20 to 0.09) or muscle growth (-0.01, -0.16 to 0.18). {schumann2022}
> The gain that did suffer was explosive strength (-0.28, -0.48 to -0.08), more so when both were done in the same session than when sessions were at least 3 hours apart. {schumann2022}
So strength and muscle gains generally hold up. If explosive work matters to you, keep it apart from cardio when you can.

## One main goal
This is practical guidance. Choose one main goal, and let it have first claim on your training days. Add other kinds of training at a smaller, steady dose. If you run out of days, trim the smaller dose first. Levlofit lets you rank your goals, and your plan shows you how they fit your week.

## Speed before fatigue
Skill and speed work is best done while you are fresh. In practice:
- Put jumps, sprints and new skills early in a session.
- Place them before your most tiring session of the week when you can.
- Do not stack them straight after a hard session on the same day.

## Lighter weeks
You do not need a separate lighter-week schedule for each kind of training. Reduce your whole week together. Hard interval or station sessions count as fatigue too, even though they are not lifting. See [[deload-week|Deload week]].

## Health minimums
Whatever your goal, WHO recommends all adults do 150 to 300 minutes a week of moderate aerobic activity, or 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous activity, or a mix, along with regular muscle-strengthening activity. {who2020}

## Key takeaways
- Combined training did not meaningfully reduce strength or muscle gains.
- Explosive strength may suffer, especially in the same session.
- Pick one main goal, and trim the rest first.
- Do speed and skill work when you are fresh.
- Aim for WHO's activity guidelines too.

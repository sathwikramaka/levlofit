## Chapter overview
Body weight is the cheapest measurement in this Handbook, and among the most misread. A decent scale has little error, while your body's daily changes are large. That combination decides everything below.

## The rule
> warn: Use a seven-day average
> Judge your weight by a seven-day average, not one reading. Weigh most days in the same conditions, average the last seven readings, and compare that average week to week.
Averaging works because daily swings partly cancel out. Your body does not change when you take an average. Your measurement improves.
A rolling average also updates every day, so one missed weigh-in does little harm.

## What the scale is weighing
| Part | How fast it moves |
| Body fat | Weeks to months. At 0.5 to 1% of body weight a week, an 80 kg person loses about 0.4 to 0.8 kg a week. {helms2014} |
| Muscle | Months to years. |
| Body water | Hours. Salt, heat, hormones and training move it. |
| Stored carbohydrate and its water | Days. |
| Food in your gut | Hours. |
What you want to measure moves over weeks. Other parts move within a day, sometimes by more than a real week's change.

## Daily or weekly?
> evidence: Evidence: moderate
> In the self-weighing review, daily and weekly weighing showed no significant difference in weight loss. {madigan2015}
> Self-weighing alone showed no evidence of working (-0.5 kg, -1.3 to 0.3). It helped as part of a programme. {madigan2015}
| Question | Answer |
| Does daily weighing beat weekly? | No significant difference. {madigan2015} |
| So why weigh most days? | A seven-day average needs readings. That is arithmetic, not a finding about behaviour. |
| Is weekly fine? | Yes. You lose the averaging but keep the trend over months. |
| Do I have to weigh at all? | No. Your training log is enough by itself. |

## Conditions
- Morning, after using the toilet, before eating or drinking.
- Same scale, on the same hard surface.
- Same clothing, or none.
- After a high-salt meal, large meal, long journey or alcohol, record the number and expect it to be high.
- If you menstruate, expect a monthly pattern. Comparing the same point each month may tell you more than comparing nearby weeks.

## Reading the trend
Compare this week's average with last week's. That difference is your signal.
| Average, week to week | Reading |
| Falling at about 0.5 to 1% a week, goal is fat loss | Working. Change nothing. |
| Flat for two or more weeks, goal is fat loss | Eat a little less or move a little more. Change one thing. |
| Falling faster than that, goal is fat loss | Slow down. Slower loss can better protect lean mass in leaner people. {aragon2017} |
| Rising slowly with lifts rising, goal is muscle | Fits your goal. No exact rate is established. |
| Flat while your log improves and waist falls | May be gaining muscle while losing fat. No reliable rate is established. |
| One day up by 2 kg | Tells you about yesterday. |
> warn: One week is not a trend
> Wait for two weekly averages moving the same way before changing anything. No single reading justifies changing your food or plan.

## A worked example
These are example numbers from one week in a fat-loss phase, not a target.
| Day | Reading |
| Monday | 74.0 kg |
| Tuesday | 74.4 kg |
| Wednesday | 73.9 kg |
| Thursday | 74.2 kg |
| Friday | 74.1 kg |
| Saturday | 73.8 kg |
| Sunday | 74.0 kg |
The average is 74.1 kg. Someone looking only at Tuesday thinks they gained. Someone looking only at Saturday thinks they are losing quickly. It is the same week, and only next week's average adds information.

## When to put the scale away
Three trials in the self-weighing review measured psychological harm. The two described here found no significant differences between groups, but most trials did not report on adverse effects. {madigan2015} This does not prove it is safe for you.
> warn: Stop weighing and see a clinician if
> The reading decides your mood, or whether you eat that day.
> You weigh more than once a day, or cannot comfortably skip a day.
> You respond to a high reading by skipping meals, adding training or purging.
> Food rules keep narrowing, or eating brings guilt.
> You cannot stop cutting after reaching your target.
> This chapter is optional. Your tape and log can track progress without a scale.

## Common mistakes
- **Judging progress on one reading.**
- **Comparing this week's best day with last week's worst.**
- **Changing your food after one weigh-in.**
- **Weighing at different times or on different scales.**
- **Treating one flat week as a plateau.**
- **Believing more weighing causes faster loss.** Daily was not significantly better than weekly.

## Key takeaways
- Use a seven-day average, not one reading.
- Daily and weekly weighing showed no significant difference.
- Weighing alone showed no evidence of working. It helps you steer.
- Wait two weeks before changing anything.
- If the scale stops helping you decide, put it away.

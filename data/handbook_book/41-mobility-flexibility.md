## Chapter overview
[[warmup-mobility|Warm-up and mobility]] covers what stretching does. This chapter asks the practical question: if you already lift, how much separate mobility work do you need? Often less than you might think.

## Definitions
| Term | Meaning |
| Mobility | The range you can reach and control at a joint. It needs strength through that range. |
| Flexibility | The range available at a joint, usually measured passively. |
Stretching does not make muscles longer. Fascicle length did not change after one session or many. Stiffness fell, and with repeated stretching, tolerance to the stretch rose. {ingram2025}

## Resistance training improves range of motion
> evidence: Evidence: moderate
> A review of 55 studies found that resistance training increased range of motion (effect size 0.73). It showed no significant difference from stretching (0.08, p = 0.79). Combining the two did no better than stretching alone (-0.001, p = 0.99). The authors conclude that stretching before or after resistance training may not be necessary to improve flexibility. {alizadeh2023}
> Untrained and sedentary people gained more (1.042) than trained or active people (0.43). Using only body mass did not significantly improve range. {alizadeh2023}
> tip: What this means for you
> If you train with external load through a full range, a separate stretching routine may add little. Full range on your working sets can also be part of your mobility work, so cutting depth to add load costs you twice.

## When dedicated mobility work is worth it
- **A position you cannot reach limits an exercise you want,** such as squat depth or a comfortable overhead position.
- **You cannot yet load the range at all.** You may need to open it first.
- **You train only with bodyweight.** The result above may not apply to you. {alizadeh2023}
- **You want range for something outside lifting.** A sport, an activity or comfort. That is a fair personal reason.
Otherwise, a general daily stretching routine is a habit you may enjoy, not something you need.

## Timing
| Approach | Effect on jumping and sprinting |
| Dynamic alone | Improved jump height and sprint time. {li2023} |
| Static, then dynamic | Improved jump height. {li2023} |
| Static alone, just before | Made sprint time worse. {li2023} |
These studies measured explosive tasks, so the effect on an ordinary set may differ.
**In practice:** if a static stretch helps you reach a position, use it, then do dynamic movement and warm-up sets. Without such a limit, skip it.

## Where limits matter
| Area | What it can limit | Standing |
| Hips | Squat depth, hinge position, lunges | Mechanically clear. Specific drills are not tested here. |
| Ankles | Squat depth and knee path | Not established that ankle drills change results. Test your own depth. |
| Shoulders and upper back | Overhead positions, pressing setup | Mechanically clear. Specific drills are not tested here. |
| Hamstrings | Hip hinge depth | Often stiffness and tolerance. Loaded hinging through a full range is a good place to start. {alizadeh2023} |

## How to train a limit
- **Pick a routine you will keep up.** One review found hold time and frequency played little role in how much range people gained. {konrad2024}
- **Load the range you gain,** so you can use it. {alizadeh2023}
- **Target the real limit.** A muscle can feel tight without being what limits you.
- **Expect months.** A warm-up uses range you already have.
Here is a practical example, if your squat depth is limited and nothing hurts:
| Step | What to do |
| Test | Film your depth from the side, with the camera in the same place each time. |
| Load what you have | Squat under load to your full available depth, every session. |
| Add targeted work if needed | Add ankle and hip work before the session if it lets you reach further that day. |
| Retest | After six to eight weeks, not weekly. |
If your depth has not moved after a couple of months, ask a qualified professional whether something structural is limiting you.

## What this chapter does not claim
> warn: Mobility and injury
> It is **not established** that mobility work prevents injury. The stronger evidence for lower injury risk is strength training itself, in sports groups. {lauersen2018} Train mobility because it lets you do exercises you otherwise could not.
> warn: Stretching into pain
> Mild discomfort is expected. Sharp, radiating or joint-line pain is not. Stop, and see a qualified clinician rather than stretching harder.

## Common mistakes
- **Doing a general daily routine and expecting more range.** If you already lift through a full range, it may add little. {alizadeh2023}
- **Cutting range on working sets, then stretching to fix it.** Your working sets could be doing that job.
- **Stretching whatever feels tight.** Tightness is a feeling, not a diagnosis.
- **Testing weekly.** Change takes months.
- **Expecting a warm-up to build range.** It uses what you have.
- **Forcing a painful position.** Stop instead.

## Key takeaways
- Resistance training with load improved range of motion about as well as stretching.
- Full range on your working sets helps your mobility.
- Stretching lowers stiffness and raises tolerance. It does not lengthen muscle.
- Dedicated work suits a specific limit, bodyweight-only training or goals outside lifting.
- Avoid static stretching alone right before explosive work.
- It is not established that mobility work prevents injury.

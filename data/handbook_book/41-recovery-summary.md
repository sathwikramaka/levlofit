## What this volume established
Much of the recovery industry addresses how recovered you feel. Very little of it has been shown to change what you get from training, and one popular tool has evidence of making strength gains smaller.
Two things carried real weight: sleep, and how much training you schedule in the first place.

## In order of evidence
| How firm | What | Why |
| Well supported | Regular, sufficient sleep | Consensus on duration, and a measured cost from sleep loss. {aasm2015} {craven2022} |
| Well supported | Enough training volume, and not too much | Volume drives results, and recovery has a cost. {pelland2025} |
| Supported | Loaded, full-range training for mobility | Moderate evidence. {alizadeh2023} |
| Supported | Protecting your attendance from stress | The main documented effect of stress. {stults2014} |
| Basic | Enough food, protein and water | Your body needs them. See the Nutrition volume. |
| Comfort | Massage, compression, heat, foam rolling | Soreness effects, from a weak literature. {wiecha2025} |
| Avoid as a routine | Cold-water immersion after lifting | Harmful effect on strength. {malta2021} |

## Daily basics
- A sleep window you keep on most nights.
- Your protein target.
- Water, without any ceremony.
- The sessions you planned, at the volume you planned.
- Movement on non-training days, if you want it.
This list does not include a gadget, a supplement or a stretching routine, because this volume could not justify any of them as daily requirements.

## If you want a structure
Pick one habit from that list, the one you would actually keep, and give it about two months. Thirty-day challenges with fixed lists end around when the median person is only halfway to a habit. Self-selected habits also generally do better than assigned ones. {smith2024}

## Before the next volume
The next volume is about physique. It is the part of training most exposed to marketing and comparison, so take two things with you:
- Feeling recovered and being recovered are different. Looking like progress and making progress are too.
- Appearance is a mixed predictor of whether people keep training. {teixeira2012}

summary: How to build a pulling day from principles, not a list.

## What a pull session trains
A pull session trains the muscles that draw weight toward you: lats, middle and upper back, rear shoulders, biceps and forearms.
Back training is often sold as a fix for desk posture. It is **not established** that strengthening your back changes your usual posture, or that doing so would bring a health or performance benefit. Train your back because it is a large part of your muscle, because balanced development is a fair goal, and because pulling strength is worth having.

## Slots
A pull session uses slots too, for the same reasons as [[push-sessions|Building a push session]]: order helps strength, and different exercises may develop different parts of a muscle. {acsm2026} {burke2025}
Include both vertical pulling (pulldowns, pull-ups) and horizontal pulling (rows), because they load your back along different lines. "Pulldowns for width, rows for thickness" is gym shorthand that no study measured. Do both for coverage.
| Slot | Job |
| Main pull | The heaviest, most technical pull, done first. |
| Second pull | The pattern the main pull did not use. |
| Lat work | A machine or cable movement through a full range. |
| Rear shoulder | Direct work, because pulling gives it limited work. |
| Biceps | Direct arm work, once your back is no longer the limit. |

## Choosing an exercise
The same four tests apply: equipment, joints, skill and fatigue. Three points are specific to pulling:
- A pull-up or barbell row asks more skill and grip than a machine row or pulldown. With less experience or energy, the supported option is a fair choice.
- **Grip can end the set first.** If your hands give out before your back, your back has not done the work you intended. Straps, a different grip or a machine all solve this.
- The main slot is where a hard, technical movement is cheapest, because you are freshest.

## Swapping exercises
A wide-grip pulldown and a pull-up can replace each other. Both are vertical pulls with the same joint actions, though the pull-up asks more stability. A seated cable row cannot replace either. It is a horizontal pull, so it belongs in the second slot.

## Volume, effort and frequency
The evidence in [[push-sessions|Building a push session]] applies unchanged.
One point matters more here: **biceps get work from every pulling set**. Counted as half a set each, heavy rows and pulldowns already give your biceps real volume before your first curl. Counting indirect sets this way predicted results best in one large review. {pelland2025}
If your arms are stalling or always sore, look at your total pulling sets before you add curls.

## How to progress
The three models from the push chapter apply. Pulling adds one wrinkle: bodyweight moves go up in big steps. You cannot add a small amount to a pull-up as you can on a pulldown, so add repetitions first and only add weight when your range is full. Assisted versions allow finer steps.

## Warm-up
Use the same three parts, aimed at your shoulders, upper back and grip. Warm-up sets matter most.

## Across the week
A pull session shares recovery with anything that loads your biceps, forearms or the back of your body, including heavy Romanian deadlifts on leg day. Plan by the muscles you actually trained, using the signals in [[recovery|Recovery]], not a fixed number of hours.

## Worked example
This is one example, not a prescription.
| Slot | Exercise | Sets | Repetitions | Rest |
| Main pull (vertical) | Pull-up or assisted pull-up | 3 | 6 to 10 | 2 to 3 min |
| Second pull (horizontal) | Chest-supported row | 3 | 8 to 10 | 2 min |
| Lat work | Neutral-grip lat pulldown | 3 | 10 to 12 | 90 sec |
| Rear shoulder | Face pull | 3 | 12 to 15 | 60 to 90 sec |
| Biceps | Incline dumbbell curl | 3 | 10 to 12 | 75 to 90 sec |
| Biceps | Hammer curl | 3 | 10 to 12 | 75 to 90 sec |
Your upper arm stays behind your body on an incline curl, which loads the biceps at a longer length. Partial repetitions at long muscle lengths gave more growth than those at short lengths in one review, though that does not directly test this exercise. {strey2026}
Six direct biceps sets look modest until you count the nine pulling sets at half each. That makes about 10.5 biceps sets from this session.

## Common mistakes
- **Swinging.** It moves the load onto your lower back and hips.
- **Pulling with your arms.** Start the movement from your shoulder blades.
- **Using more load than your grip can hold.** The set ends before your back has worked.
- **Skipping rear shoulder work.** It needs its own slot.
- **Adding curls to fix stalled arms.** Check your total pulling sets first.
- **Using one pattern for both pull slots.** Two rows or two pulldowns cover only one line of pull.

## Key takeaways
- Cover both vertical and horizontal pulling.
- Back training does not need a posture claim to justify it.
- Every pulling set also trains your biceps. Count it as half.
- Grip limits are real. Solve them instead of pushing through.
- Recovery follows the muscles you trained, not the name of the session.

## Chapter overview
Strength is a skill as well as muscle. Lift heavy, practise the same lifts often, and rest long enough to lift well.

## Load and rest
Heavy loads build more maximal strength than light loads. {schoenfeld2017l}
> evidence: Evidence: contested for how long to rest
> One review found strength improves even with short rests, with trained lifters gaining the most from rests over 2 minutes and beginners needing only 1 to 2 minutes. {grgic2018} The ACSM found no strength difference between rest under and over one minute. {acsm2026}
In practice, rest long enough that your next heavy set is a good one. That is often 2 minutes or more on the big lifts.

## The main lifts
| Lift | What it trains | Key cue |
| Squat | Legs, hips, trunk | Brace, knees track over toes |
| Bench press | Chest, shoulders, triceps | Shoulder blades pinned, feet planted |
| Deadlift | Back, hips, grip | Bar close, back flat, push the floor away |
| Overhead press | Shoulders, triceps | Glutes tight, bar over mid-foot |

## How hard to push
This is a Levlofit rule, using the reps in reserve scale. {zourdos2016}
| Reps in reserve | Used for | When |
| 4 or more | Technique | Warm-up and practice sets |
| 2 to 3 | Most work sets | Bar speed stays good |
| 0 to 1 | Rarely | Tests and the last set of a block |

## A strength block
- **Weeks 1 to 3.** Build: add 2.5 to 5 kg when every rep is clean.
- **Week 4.** A lighter week to recover.
- **Then.** Retest, or start the next block a little heavier.
> warn: Powerlifting meets
> Powerlifting meets need a coach for technique and peaking. Levlofit's Strength path builds the base.

## Key takeaways
- Heavy loads build more maximal strength than light loads.
- Rest long enough for a good next set, often 2 minutes or more on heavy lifts.
- Build for 3 weeks, take a lighter week, then retest.

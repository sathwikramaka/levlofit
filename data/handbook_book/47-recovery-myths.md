## Chapter overview
This chapter adds no new evidence. Each answer repeats a finding from this volume. If an answer seems thin, that is because the evidence is thin.

## The myth that costs the most
> evidence: "Ice baths speed up recovery, so they must help."
> Regular cold-water immersion at 15 °C or below had a harmful effect on strength adaptations from training (effect size -0.60, -0.87 to -0.33), and did not appear to affect aerobic performance. {malta2021}
> Eight articles is a small base. But this is the only recovery tool in this volume with evidence of making a training result worse.

## Recovery myths
| Claim | What this volume found |
| "If I am sore, it was a good session." | Soreness mainly reflects unfamiliar work, and it fades with repetition. |
| "Recovery tools speed up recovery." | Several reduce soreness, on weak evidence. {wiecha2025} Faster recovery and better results are not established here, and cold immersion harmed strength. {malta2021} |
| "Stretching prevents injury." | Not established here. Strength training has stronger evidence, in sports groups. {lauersen2018} |
| "Stretching lengthens muscles." | No. Fascicle length did not change. Stiffness fell, and tolerance rose with repeated stretching. {ingram2025} |
| "You need a stretching routine for mobility." | Resistance training improved range about as well as stretching. Using only body mass did not significantly help. {alizadeh2023} |
| "Catch up on sleep at the weekend." | The recommendation is regular sleep. {aasm2015} Full catch-up is not established. |
| "Poor sleep will get you injured." | Current evidence does not support it as an independent risk factor. {dobrosielski2021} |
| "Rest days are wasted days." | Recovery is part of training. |
| "Exercise is the cure for stress." | Stress predicted less activity in most prospective studies. {stults2014} Exercise may help your mood, and it does not replace care. {noetel2024} |
| "It takes 21 days to build a habit." | Median times were 59 to 66 days, with individuals from 4 to 335 days. {smith2024} |

## Common questions
| Question | Answer |
| How many rest days should I take? | No fixed number is given here. Plan by the training you can recover from. |
| Can I train if I am sore? | Generally yes, if your technique is normal. If it changes how you move, train something else. |
| Do recovery gadgets work? | Some treatments reduce soreness. {wiecha2025} Better results are not established. |
| Should I stretch every day? | Only for a specific limit, bodyweight-only training or a goal outside lifting. {alizadeh2023} |
| Is it okay to take a week off? | Yes. Guidance on lighter weeks comes from expert agreement, not experiments. {bell2023} |
| How much water do I need? | No one number fits everyone. See [[hydration|Hydration and electrolytes]]. |
| How long until a habit feels automatic? | Often about two months. {smith2024} |

## A quick guide
| Situation | Reasonable response |
| Slept badly last night | Train if you can, expect it to feel harder, and make tonight's sleep the priority. {craven2022} |
| Mild soreness | Train normally. |
| Soreness that changes how you move | Train something else today. |
| Flat for a session or two | Check sleep, stress, volume and food. |
| Flat for weeks, beyond training | This is not a training question. See a qualified clinician. |
| Sharp, radiating or joint-line pain during a lift | Stop that exercise and get it checked. |
| Tempted by an ice bath after heavy lifting | Do not make it a routine if strength is your goal. {malta2021} |

## Common mistakes
- Buying a tool before fixing your sleep.
- Reading "reduces soreness" as "improves recovery".
- Making cold-water immersion a routine because it feels useful.
- Treating "not established" as "disproven".
- Waiting to feel recovered instead of reading your log.

## Key takeaways
- Sleep and training volume are the two recovery levers with real weight.
- Less soreness and better results are separate outcomes.
- One tool has evidence of harming strength gains.
- Several popular recovery claims are not established, and this volume says so.

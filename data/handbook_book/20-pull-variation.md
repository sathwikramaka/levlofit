## Chapter overview
[[push-variation|Changing push exercises]] sets out the position: variation is not required. {acsm2026} This chapter gives a second good way to fill the pull slots, and says when that is worth the cost.

## Reasons specific to pulling
- **Grip and forearms.** If your hands limit the set before your back does, move to machine-supported pulling or use straps.
- **Coverage.** Different lines of pull work your back differently, and different exercises may develop different parts of a muscle. The direct evidence is from legs, so this is reasoning. {burke2025}
- **Bodyweight limits.** A pull-up goes up in big steps and may be out of reach at first. A pulldown adjusts finely in both directions. Switching is a loading decision.
Without one of these or another good reason, keep what you are still progressing on.

## What stays the same
The slots from [[pull-sessions|Building a pull session]] stay. Volume, effort, frequency, progression and warm-up do not change with your exercise choice.

## Applying the swap test
- **Main pull:** wide-grip lat pulldown instead of a pull-up. Same vertical pattern and joint action, less stability demand and much finer loading steps.
- **Second pull:** seated cable row instead of chest-supported row. Same horizontal pattern. Your free torso adds light trunk work, but also makes it easier to swing.
- **Other slots:** single-arm dumbbell row, reverse pec deck, EZ-bar curl and cable curl each work in their slots.
A cable curl with your elbow behind your body, or an incline curl, loads your biceps at a longer length than a standing EZ-bar curl. The evidence favouring longer lengths compared partial ranges and is indirect here. {strey2026}

## Worked example
This is one example, not a prescription.
| Slot | Exercise | Sets | Repetitions | Rest |
| Main pull (vertical) | Wide-grip lat pulldown | 4 | 8 to 10 | 2 to 3 min |
| Second pull (horizontal) | Seated cable row | 3 | 8 to 10 | 2 min |
| Lat work | Single-arm dumbbell row | 3 | 10 to 12 | 90 sec |
| Rear shoulder | Reverse pec deck | 3 | 12 to 15 | 60 to 90 sec |
| Biceps | EZ-bar curl | 3 | 8 to 10 | 75 to 90 sec |
| Biceps | Cable curl | 3 | 12 to 15 | 60 to 90 sec |
As before, six direct sets understate your biceps work. The ten pulling sets add about five more when counted at half each. {pelland2025}

## Common mistakes
- **Changing on a schedule.** It costs your reference numbers and buys nothing the evidence can identify.
- **Swinging your body.** A free-torso row makes this easy. It shifts work to your hips and lower back.
- **Pulling with your arms.** Your arms already get indirect work.
- **Shortening your range to add load.** On pulls, this usually loses the stretch, the part worth keeping.
- **Losing rear shoulder work when you change exercises.** Check that the slot is still filled.
- **Adding load before you have learned a new variation.** Coordination comes first.

## Key takeaways
- Variation is optional. Change for grip, coverage, loading steps, a true stall or honest motivation.
- Machine pulling lets you work your back hard with less grip fatigue. It is a real trade, not a lesser option.
- Rear shoulder work is the slot most easily lost. Check for it.
- When you shorten a pull, the stretch is usually what goes. Protect it.

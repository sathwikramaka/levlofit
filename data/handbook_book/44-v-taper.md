## Chapter overview
The V-taper is the contrast between your shoulder and upper-back width and your waist. It is a popular goal, often sold with claims that do not hold up.
Wanting one is an aesthetic preference, not a health or performance target. This chapter is about what is achievable, not whether it is worth achieving.

## Three contributors, two of them trainable
| Contributor | Trainable? | Status |
| Skeleton: shoulder and hip width, rib cage | No, in adults | Bone width does not widen with training once you have finished growing. |
| Muscle over the frame: shoulders, lats, upper back | Yes | Ordinary muscle growth. {pelland2025} |
| Body fat, especially at the waist | Yes, but not selectively | You cannot train your waist lean. |
So two people with the same plan and effort can end up with different tapers. Much of that difference is likely to be skeletal, and it is no failure of motivation.

## Building the muscle
What drives growth in your shoulders and lats is the same as anywhere else: enough weekly work directed at the muscle, progressed over time, with diminishing returns as sets rise. {pelland2025}

## Exercise choice: reasonable, not decisive
The claim that one exercise builds one part of a muscle has thin support. The direct evidence here is one lower-body study. {burke2025} Doing exercises that load a muscle in different positions is sensible and possibly useful. None of these is required.
| Target | Usual choices |
| Shoulders | Overhead presses and lateral raises |
| Lats | Pull-ups and pulldowns |
| Upper back | Supported and cable rows |
| Rear shoulders | Face pulls and reverse flys |
| Upper chest | Incline presses |

## The waist: three common claims
| Claim | Position |
| "Heavy core and oblique work thickens your waist, so avoid it." | Not established. This is a belief, and no reason to skip trunk work. |
| "Vacuums and hollowing narrow your waist." | Not established. |
| "Train your abs to lose the fat over them." | Do not rely on it. Overall fat loss is what changes this. |
What really changes how your waist looks is your overall body fat, which is neither quick nor selective.
> tip: Measure your waist, not a ratio
> Use a tape at the same spot every few weeks. Shoulder-to-waist targets online, including the "golden ratio", have no validated basis, so this Handbook gives none.

## Common mistakes
- **Expecting training to widen your bones.** It adds muscle over them.
- **Skipping trunk work to protect your waist.** That belief is not established.
- **Building a plan around sculpting regions.** The evidence is thin.
- **Training your abs to reveal them.** That comes from overall fat loss.
- **Adding shoulder and back sets without cutting elsewhere.** It costs recovery.
- **Chasing a published ratio.** There is no validated target.

## Key takeaways
- Bone is fixed, muscle responds to training, and fat cannot be lost selectively.
- Shoulders and lats grow on the same dose-response as anything else.
- Vary your exercises sensibly, but do not plan around sculpting.
- Your waist responds to overall fat loss.
- Two people with the same effort will still look different.

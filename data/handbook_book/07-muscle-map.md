## Chapter overview
Knowing which muscle does what makes every plan easier to read: why a session has the moves it has, and what to swap in when you cannot do one. Names here follow standard anatomy. {openstax2022}

## Upper body
| Muscle group | What it does | Main moves | Smaller moves |
| Chest (pectorals) | Pushes the arms forward and across the body | Bench press, push-up, dips | Cable or dumbbell fly |
| Front shoulders (anterior deltoid) | Raises the arm forward and overhead | Overhead press, incline press | Front raise |
| Side shoulders (lateral deltoid) | Raises the arm out to the side; makes the shoulders look wider | Overhead press (partly) | Lateral raise, cable lateral raise |
| Rear shoulders (posterior deltoid) | Pulls the arm back | Rows | Rear-delt fly, face pull |
| Upper back (lats) | Pulls the arms down and back; the V-shape | Pull-up, chin-up, pulldown | Straight-arm pulldown, pullover |
| Mid back (rhomboids, middle trapezius) | Squeezes the shoulder blades together | Rows (barbell, dumbbell, cable) | Reverse fly |
| Upper trapezius | Lifts the shoulders | Deadlift, carries | Shrug |
| Biceps | Bends the elbow and turns the palm up | Chin-up, rows | Curls (standing, incline, preacher) |
| Brachialis and forearm | Bends the elbow with the palm sideways or down | Rows | Hammer curl, reverse curl |
| Triceps | Straightens the elbow | Bench press, dips, close-grip push-up | Overhead extension, pushdown |
| Forearms and grip | Close the hand and move the wrist | Deadlift, carries, hangs | Wrist curl, reverse wrist curl |

## Lower body and trunk
| Muscle group | What it does | Main moves | Smaller moves |
| Quadriceps (front thigh) | Straightens the knee | Squat, leg press, lunge, split squat | Leg extension |
| Hamstrings (back thigh) | Bends the knee and extends the hip | Romanian deadlift, deadlift | Leg curl, Nordic curl |
| Glutes | Extends the hip; keeps the pelvis level | Squat, hip thrust, deadlift, lunge | Glute bridge, kickback |
| Adductors (inner thigh) | Bring the legs together | Sumo squat, wide stance squat | Adductor machine, Copenhagen plank |
| Abductors (outer hip) | Move the leg out; steady the hip on one leg | Single-leg work, lunges | Band walks, abductor machine |
| Calves | Point the foot; push off when walking and running | Running, jumping | Standing calf raise (gastrocnemius), seated calf raise (soleus) |
| Abs (rectus abdominis) | Curl the trunk; brace against bending back | Heavy squats and deadlifts (bracing) | Crunch, leg raise, ab wheel |
| Obliques | Twist and bend the trunk sideways; resist twisting | Carries, single-arm work | Pallof press, side plank, cable woodchop |
| Lower back (erectors) | Keep the spine straight under load | Deadlift, squat, rows | Back extension, good morning |

## Push, pull and legs
:: plate How a full week splits the body | -
Push: chest, front and side shoulders, triceps | 0.33 | bench, overhead press, dips
Pull: back, rear shoulders, biceps, grip | 0.33 | rows, pull-ups, curls
Legs and trunk: quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves, abs | 0.34 | squat, hinge, lunge, core
::
Every balanced plan covers all three. A rough rule: about as many pulling sets as pushing sets, which keeps shoulders comfortable and posture balanced. See [[push-sessions|Building a push session]], [[pull-sessions|Building a pull session]] and [[leg-sessions|Building a leg session]].

## When you cannot do a move
Pick another move from the same row: same muscle, same job, different tool. For example, no pull-up bar: a pulldown or band-assisted pull-up. No barbell: dumbbell or machine versions. Levlofit's Workout tab lists other moves for each exercise, and Levlofit AI can make a swap stick.

## A muscle that lags behind
:: steps Bringing up one muscle
Check the basics first | Are you eating enough and progressing on your main lifts? A muscle cannot grow without both.
Train it more often | Add 2 to 4 hard sets for it on one more day a week.
Add a direct move | If it only gets compound work, add an isolation move for it near the end of a session.
Try other rep ranges | Lighter sets of 15 to 30 reps or heavier sets of 6 to 8, taken close to failure.
Be patient | Small muscles (calves, forearms, side shoulders) change slowest; judge over 8 to 12 weeks.
::
Occasionally the fix is less work, not more, when recovery is the problem; that is the less common case.

## Left and right are different
Small differences between sides are normal and rarely matter. For a clear gap:
- Use single-arm and single-leg moves, starting with the weaker side.
- Let the weaker side set the reps and weight; the stronger side matches, no more.
- Film your bilateral lifts to stop leaning or twisting toward the stronger side.
See [[lagging-muscles|Lagging muscles]] and [[posture|Posture and movement]].

## Key takeaways
- Every muscle group has a main move and a few smaller moves; any one from the same row trains it.
- Balance pushing and pulling across the week.
- For a lagging muscle: eat enough, then add sets or a direct move, and give it 8 to 12 weeks.

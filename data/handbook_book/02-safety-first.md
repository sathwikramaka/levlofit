## Chapter overview
Most adults can start exercising today. A small group should speak to a doctor first, and everyone should know the few signs that mean stop.

## Should you see a doctor first?
Screening rests on three things: how active you are now, any signs or known disease, and how hard you plan to train. {riebe2015} Go through these questions in order.
| Question | If yes | If no |
| Do you have chest pain, fainting, unusual breathlessness, a racing heartbeat or ankle swelling? | Stop here. See a doctor before exercising. | Go to the next question. |
| Has a doctor told you that you have heart, kidney or metabolic disease such as diabetes? | Get medical clearance first, then start light and build slowly. | Go to the next question. |
| Are you mostly inactive right now, exercising fewer than 3 days a week? | Start at light to moderate effort and build up over weeks. | Carry on, and build intensity gradually. |

## Normal, or a reason to stop
| Normal during training | Stop and get help |
| Muscles burning near the end of a set | Chest pain, pressure or tightness |
| Breathing hard, then recovering within a minute or two | Dizziness, faintness or confusion |
| Muscle soreness a day or two later | A heartbeat that feels irregular or racing |
| | Sharp or sudden joint pain |

## Start low
Some activity is better than none, even if you cannot reach the target yet. If you have been inactive, start low and build slowly. {who2020}
> warn: Follow your doctor's advice first
> Pregnant, recovering from surgery, or managing a long-term condition? Follow your doctor's advice first. Levlofit will ask you and adjust.

## Key takeaways
- Most adults can start today; some should see a doctor first.
- Chest pain, fainting, dizziness or an irregular heartbeat mean stop and get help.
- Some activity is better than none. Start low and build slowly.

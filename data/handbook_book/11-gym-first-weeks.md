## Chapter overview
The first weeks decide whether you keep going. Most worries beginners have are common, normal and easy to handle once you know what to expect.

## Feeling self-conscious
:: compare What it feels like | What is usually true
Everyone is watching me. | Most people are focused on their own sets and their phone.
They are judging how little I lift. | Every strong person started light; many remember being new.
I do not belong here. | A gym is for anyone who trains, at any level.
::
:: steps Making the first visits easier
Go at quiet hours | Mid-morning or early afternoon is calmer than 6 to 8 pm.
Arrive with a plan | Open today's session in Levlofit so you always know the next move.
Start with what you know | Machines and dumbbells first; add barbell lifts as you learn them.
Keep going back | Comfort comes from repetition: by the fourth or fifth visit it feels normal.
::

## Gym manners
- Re-rack your weights and wipe down benches and handles after use.
- Do not stand directly in front of someone's mirror view or rest on a machine between long phone breaks.
- Ask "how many sets left?" before working in on equipment.
- Do not give advice to people who did not ask, even about form. You do not know their goals, injuries or programme, and it usually comes across as rude. If someone is putting others in danger, tell the staff.

## Learning a new lift
You do not need a trainer for every lift. Many people learn well from good videos and practice.
:: steps Learning a lift on your own
Watch | Watch a clear demonstration (Levlofit's exercise library shows each move) from front and side.
Practise light | Start with an empty bar, light dumbbells or a bodyweight version.
Film yourself | Record from the side. What you feel and what you do are often different.
Compare | Check your video against the demonstration and fix one thing at a time.
Get help when stuck | A coach for one session is worth it if a lift hurts or stays wrong after a few weeks.
::
See [[technique|Learning a lift]].

## Coming back after a break
Strength and muscle come back faster than they took to build the first time, but your joints and tendons need a week or two to catch up.
:: band Weight to start with after a break | % of your old working weight | -
0 | 50 | Break of 6 months or more: start here and add weight each session | good
50 | 70 | Break of 1 to 6 months | good
70 | 90 | Break of 2 to 4 weeks | ok
90 | 100 | Break under 2 weeks: pick up where you left off | ok
::
These are Levlofit's starting rules, not research limits. Expect to be back near your old numbers within weeks. Beginner-style fast progress is not something you can "waste"; if you are weak now, you will improve fast now.

## Bad days happen
One weak session means little: poor sleep, a heavy or empty stomach, stress, dehydration or simply a bad day. Everyone has them, from beginners to elite lifters. Log it, go home, and come back for the next planned session. Look for a cause only when sessions are poor for two weeks or more. See [[plateaus|Breaking through plateaus]].

## Training when you are ill
:: flow Should I train today?
Fever, body aches, chest congestion or stomach illness? | Rest. Train again 1 to 2 days after the fever is gone. | Go on to the next question.
Only a runny nose or mild sore throat? | Light training is usually fine; drop the weight and stop if you feel worse. | Train as planned.
::
This is common practical advice, not a research rule. If you are contagious, stay home for the sake of everyone else, and wipe down what you touch. Any chest pain, fainting or unusual breathlessness means stop and see a doctor.

## Is it soreness or an injury?
:: compare Probably normal soreness | Possibly an injury
Starts 24 to 48 hours after training | Starts during the session, often suddenly
A dull, spread-out ache in the muscle | Sharp, in one spot, or in a joint
Eases over 2 to 4 days | Lasts more than about 4 days, or gets worse
Moving gently helps | Swelling, bruising, clicking with pain, or weakness
::
Train through mild soreness with a good warm-up; it usually eases as you move. Not being sore does not mean the session failed: soreness only means something was new. See [[muscle-soreness|Muscle soreness]] and [[injury-prevention|Injury prevention]]. See a doctor or physiotherapist for anything in the right-hand column.

## When your grip gives out first
On deadlifts and rows, your grip often fails before your back or legs. Your grip should not decide how heavy you train those lifts.
:: steps Fixes, in order
Chalk | Dry hands hold far better; liquid chalk is allowed in more gyms.
Grip the bar low | Hold it in the fingers near the palm crease, not in the middle of the palm, to stop skin pinching.
Hook or mixed grip | Thumb under the fingers, or one palm forward and one back, for heavy sets.
Straps | For your heaviest sets; strong lifters use them without shame.
Train grip directly | Dead hangs, farmer's carries and heavy holds, once or twice a week.
::

## Key takeaways
- Nerves fade with repetition; go at quiet hours with a plan.
- Learn lifts with videos, light weights and filming yourself.
- After a break, start lighter and build back for a week or two.
- One bad session means nothing; rest when you have a fever or chest symptoms.
- Sharp pain during a session is not soreness: stop and get it checked.

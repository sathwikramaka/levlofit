## Chapter overview
This is where your numbers turn into decisions. Mostly, its job is to stop you. In most months, the right action is to change nothing, and that is the review working. Levlofit's weekly report gives you the raw material.

## The evidence here
> evidence: Evidence: moderate
> Across ten studies of habit programmes, problem solving was linked with better results (0.36, 0.17 to 0.55). Action planning, self-monitoring and prompts were common. {ma2023}
Problem solving means working out what usually stops you, and deciding what you will do when it happens. The rest of this chapter is reasoning from the measurement errors in this volume.

## Why most months should end in no change
| Reason | What it means |
| Your tools are noisy | Much month-to-month movement is measurement, not you. |
| Reviewing many numbers guarantees a bad one | With six noisy numbers, at least one is likely to look alarming by chance. |
> warn: Choose your decision number before you look
> Decide in advance which one number decides things, such as your seven-day weight average when losing fat or your log when gaining. Treat the others as context, so you cannot go looking for the number that justifies what you already wanted to do.

## Decision rules
| What your number shows | Action |
| Moving as intended | **Change nothing.** |
| Weight average flat for two or more weeks, goal is fat loss | Eat a little less or move a little more. **Change one thing.** |
| Weight falling too fast | Slow down. Slower loss can better protect lean mass in leaner people. {aragon2017} |
| Two or three sessions of a lift flat or falling | Check sleep, food, stress and volume before changing your plan. |
| Several lifts flat for weeks, with those in order | See [[plateaus|Breaking through plateaus]] and [[deload-week|Deload week]]. |
| Tape or photos disagree with a rising log | Trust your log. |
| Lasting pain or red-flag symptoms | Not a training decision. See a clinician. |
| Signs of low energy availability or body image distress | **Stop this phase and see a clinician.** No physique target overrides this. |
> tip: Change one thing
> If you change your training and your food in the same month, your next review cannot tell you which one mattered.

## What to review, in order
| Order | What to ask |
| 1. Attendance | Did your plan actually happen? A month of missed sessions does not test your plan. |
| 2. Your training log | Loads and reps per lift, across the month. |
| 3. Your decision number | The one you chose in advance. |
| 4. Inputs | Sleep, stress and food, to explain what you saw. |
| 5. Photos, every few months | Skip the months when a comparison would only be noise. |
| 6. One obstacle, and a plan for it | The problem-solving step. {ma2023} |
Avoid rating things like "satisfaction" out of five and tracking them as if they were measurements. Attendance can be counted. Satisfaction cannot.

## Motivation
Noticing that you got better at something supports your sense of competence, which is linked with continuing to exercise. {teixeira2012} Keep that. Do not treat this review as a test you pass or fail.

## Common mistakes
- **Reviewing six numbers and reacting to the worst.**
- **Choosing your decision number after looking.**
- **Changing training and food in the same month.**
- **Reading one month of tape or photos as a trend.**
- **Reviewing a month when your plan did not happen.**
- **Treating "no change needed" as a failed review.**
- **Making a training decision about pain.**

## Key takeaways
- Most reviews should end in no change.
- Choose your decision number before you look.
- Wait two weeks, or two or three sessions, before acting.
- Change one thing.
- Plan for what usually stops you.
- Pain and safety signs are not training decisions.

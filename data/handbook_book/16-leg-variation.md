## Chapter overview
Variation is not required, as [[push-variation|Changing push exercises]] explains. {acsm2026} This chapter gives a second good way to fill the leg slots, and covers single-leg training.

## Reasons specific to legs
- **Spine loading.** A barbell back squat loads your spine in a way a hack squat or leg press does not. A lower-back limit is a concrete reason to change your main lift.
- **Coverage.** Different exercises developed different parts of the quadriceps in a study of exactly these muscles. {burke2025}
- **Equipment.** Leg training depends heavily on what your gym has.
Without a reason, keep the exercise you are still progressing on.

## Single-leg work
Split squats, lunges, single-leg presses and single-leg hinges are often sold as better than two-leg work. The evidence is more specific.
> evidence: Evidence: moderate for strength being specific; insufficient for a difference in growth
> A review of 9 studies found you get stronger at the version you train. Two-leg training gave more two-leg strength (effect size 0.56, 0.16 to 0.96), and single-leg training gave more single-leg strength (-0.65, -0.93 to -0.37). {kassiano2025}
> For muscle growth it found no significant difference (-0.21, -3.56 to 3.13). That interval is very wide, so this does not show the two are equal. It shows the research cannot yet tell. The authors call the literature limited. {kassiano2025}
Training one side at a time does show what each side can do. That follows from how it is done. It is **not established** that this corrects a difference between your sides better than two-leg work does.
Small side-to-side differences are common. Marked, lasting or painful ones are a reason to see a clinician, not to add more single-leg sets.
Good reasons to use single-leg work are practical: limited equipment, a spine-loading limit, preference, and seeing each side in your log.

## Applying the swap test
A swap must match the movement pattern. A Bulgarian split squat is a knee-driven squat pattern on one leg. It cannot fill the hinge slot, because that leaves your hinge undone. For a single-leg hinge, use a single-leg Romanian deadlift.
- **Main squat:** hack squat instead of barbell back squat. Same pattern, more support, and less load on your spine.
- **Hip hinge:** single-leg Romanian deadlift instead of the two-leg version. Same pattern. You trade total load for balance and a view of each side.
- **Second quad lift:** keep this multi-joint. Replacing it with a second isolation exercise may lose what it adds. {burke2025}
- **Isolation slots:** a lying leg curl and seated calf raise replace the seated curl and standing raise, changing the length at which those muscles work.

## What stays the same
Volume, effort, frequency and rest do not change with your exercise choice. No fixed recovery time is established. A set on each leg is one set per leg, not two sets. Counting it as two inflates your log without adding work to either side.

## Worked example
This is one example, not a prescription.
| Slot | Exercise | Sets | Repetitions | Rest |
| Main squat | Hack squat | 4 | 8 to 10 | 2 to 3 min |
| Hip hinge | Single-leg Romanian deadlift | 3 | 8 to 10 per leg | 90 sec |
| Second quad lift | Leg press | 3 | 10 to 12 | 90 sec |
| Hamstring isolation | Lying leg curl | 3 | 10 to 12 | 75 to 90 sec |
| Quad isolation | Leg extension | 3 | 12 to 15 | 60 to 90 sec |
| Calves | Seated calf raise | 4 | 12 to 15 | 60 to 90 sec |
The hinge slot uses an actual hinge, and the second quad slot stays multi-joint. With no hack squat, a Bulgarian split squat is a reasonable choice in the main squat slot, at a lower load.

## Common mistakes
- **Swapping across patterns.** A squat pattern does not fill a hinge slot.
- **Counting a per-leg set as two.** It inflates your log.
- **Replacing multi-joint work with more isolation.** They may add different things.
- **Cutting depth on single-leg work to add load.** Balance will limit your load first. That is expected.
- **Changing on a schedule.** It costs your reference numbers.
- **Chasing perfect symmetry.** Small differences are common.

## Key takeaways
- Variation is optional. Change for spine loading, coverage, equipment, a true stall or honest motivation.
- A swap must match the pattern. A split squat is not a hinge.
- Single-leg and two-leg work show no established difference in growth. Strength is specific to what you train.
- Single-leg work shows each side. That it corrects differences is not established.
- Keep the second quad lift multi-joint.

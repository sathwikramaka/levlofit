## Chapter overview
Restaurants, travel, weddings and festivals are a permanent part of life. A way of eating that only works in your own kitchen will not last.
The ISSN says the long-term success of a diet depends on sticking with it. {aragon2017} So how you handle the exceptions matters. The advice below is practical judgement, not research.

## Timescale is the whole argument
| Timescale | What it tells you |
| One meal | Very little, against a week or a month. |
| One day | Little on its own. |
| A week | The shortest span worth reacting to. |
| A month or more | Where your body composition really responds. |
One large restaurant meal is only a part of a week's food. The bigger cost often comes from your reaction to it.

## The "80/20 rule"
You may hear that 80% of your meals should be nutritious and 20% flexible. The idea is sound: most of your food should serve your goals, and some can be flexible. The exact ratio is a memory aid, not a finding. Build a week that mostly works, and stop auditing the exceptions.

## Eating out
You are usually choosing from someone else's options, so aim for the best available choice.
| What to do | How |
| Find the protein | Paneer, dal, chana, curd, eggs, chicken, fish or tofu. Restaurant meals often have plenty of energy and less protein. |
| Notice the oil | Deep-fried dishes and rich gravies usually have far more calories than tandoori, grilled, roasted or dry versions. |
| Count your drinks | Sugary drinks, juice and alcohol add calories with little fullness. |
| Watch portions | Sharing, or stopping when comfortable, often works better than deciding beforehand to eat half. |
| Add volume | A salad, raita or vegetable side can replace some richer food. |
> tip: Do not arrive starving
> Skipping meals to "save" calories for an event can backfire and lead to eating more. Eat normally through the day, with protein, then enjoy the event.

## Alcohol
- **It contains calories,** without much fullness, so they are easy to miss.
- **Mixers add more,** especially soft drinks and juices.
- **It can disturb your sleep,** even if it makes you drowsy.
- **It can loosen your food decisions** during and after drinking.
Levlofit makes no health recommendation about drinking. That is a personal and medical matter. If your progress has stalled and alcohol is regular, count it before you decide your plan has failed.

## Travel
- **Carry protein.** Roasted chana, peanuts, whey in a shaker, or milk cartons where available.
- **Carry water.** Travel days make it easy to drink too little.
- **Do not skip meals because the options are imperfect.** An imperfect meal usually beats a missed one.
- **Expect your weight to move for reasons other than fat.** Salt, fluid, fibre and timing all change. Your seven-day average will smooth this out.
- **Walk.** Travel is otherwise very still.
A missed week of training does not undo months of work. The bigger risk is returning at your old loads straight away, so ease back in.

## Festivals and celebrations
Indian festival seasons can be long and full of food. Treating each one as a threat is miserable and unnecessary.
- Eat normally, with protein, during the day.
- Eat what you want at the occasion.
- Return to your usual pattern at the **next meal**, not next Monday.
- Do not punish yourself with extra exercise or restriction afterwards.
> warn: Compensation is the pattern to watch
> Skipping meals before an event, exercising to "cancel" food, restricting hard the next day, or feeling a lot of guilt about a meal can turn an ordinary occasion into a problem.
> These can also be early signs of a difficult relationship with food. If the pattern continues, feels hard to control or affects your daily life, talk to a qualified clinician or dietitian. This Handbook does not diagnose or treat eating disorders.

## When choices are limited
Hostel mess, conference food, long journeys, family obligations. In order of usefulness:
- Take the protein that is available.
- Add vegetables or fruit, if there are any.
- Drink water, not sweetened drinks.
- Eat until comfortable, not full.
- Stop thinking about it afterwards.

## Common mistakes
- **Believing one meal matters.** Over a month, it matters little.
- **Skipping meals beforehand.** It can make you eat more overall.
- **Declaring a "cheat day".** One flexible meal can grow into a whole day.
- **Giving up on the week after one meal.** This costs far more than the meal.
- **Punishing yourself with exercise.** Training is not a payment for food.
- **Forgetting drinks.** They can add up quickly.
- **Reacting to the scale after travel.** Wait for your average.

## Key takeaways
- Your body responds over months. One meal is small, and your reaction can cost more.
- 80/20 is a memory aid, not a finding.
- Eating out: find the protein, notice the oil, count your drinks and do not arrive starving.
- Alcohol adds calories and can disturb your sleep.
- Travelling: carry protein and water, and ignore the scale for a few days.
- Watch for compensating. Get support if it becomes a pattern.

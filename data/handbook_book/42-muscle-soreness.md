## Chapter overview
Delayed-onset muscle soreness (DOMS) is the ache that appears a day or two after unfamiliar or hard exercise. The review below describes it as coming from intense, unaccustomed exercise. {wiecha2025} It tells you less about your training than many people assume.
This chapter separates three questions: what soreness is, what reduces it, and whether reducing it does anything for your training.

## What soreness is
Soreness usually peaks a day or two after a session, and it is most likely after unfamiliar work: a new plan, a return after a break, a new exercise or a jump in volume. Its exact cause is not settled, and nothing practical here depends on settling it.
Repeat the same session a week or two later and you will usually be less sore, even with the same or more work. So soreness mostly tells you the work was unfamiliar. It does not tell you the work was productive.

## Soreness is not a progress signal
> tip: In practice
> A productive session after months of training may leave you barely sore. A new session can leave you very sore and still give you little. Chasing soreness adds recovery cost. Judge your sessions by your logged performance over weeks.
There is also evidence behind this. In one small study, muscle damage peaked early in training and was not related to later growth. {damas2016}

## What reduces soreness
> evidence: Evidence: moderate for direction, with a serious quality warning
> A review of 29 systematic reviews, covering 863 trials and 24 treatments, reported effects on soreness at various time points from cooling therapy, cryostimulation, contrast therapy, massage, phototherapy and kinesiotaping. Evidence for stretching, exercise and electrical stimulation was weak. {wiecha2025}
> **Seventeen of the 29 reviews were of critically low quality, and only two were high quality.** The authors say the evidence has uncertainties and weaknesses. {wiecha2025}
> warn: Less pain is not the same as better results
> These findings are about soreness, a feeling. They do not show that a treatment speeds up your performance recovery or improves your results. Feeling better is a fair goal. It is just a different goal.
Probably the most practical way to limit soreness is not on those lists: **progress gradually, so the work is rarely unfamiliar.**

## What has weak support for soreness
| Common advice | Standing |
| Stretching | Weak evidence. {wiecha2025} |
| Light exercise | Weak evidence for soreness. It is still reasonable for other reasons. {wiecha2025} |
| Electrical stimulation | Weak evidence overall. {wiecha2025} |
| Warming up to prevent it | Not established. |
| Extra protein to prevent it | Not established. Protein matters for other reasons. |

## Training while sore
This table is practical judgement.
| Situation | Reasonable response |
| Mild soreness, technique normal | Train normally. |
| Moderate soreness, technique or range affected | Lower the load and keep a full range. |
| Severe soreness limiting normal movement | Train something else or cut back a lot. Then ask whether your volume rose too quickly. |
| Sore in the same area for weeks | This is unlikely to be ordinary soreness. See below. |

## Soreness or injury?
These are practical signs, not a diagnosis.
| More like soreness | Get it checked |
| Appears hours to a day or two later | Came on suddenly, during the session |
| Dull, spread across the muscle | Sharp, stabbing or in one spot |
| Better day by day | The same or getting worse |
| Both sides, if both were trained | One side or one specific point |
| Stiff but able to move | Limited movement, joint involved or swelling |
> warn: Urgent: when soreness is not soreness
> Rarely, severe muscle damage after extreme or unaccustomed exercise causes rhabdomyolysis. Muscle contents enter the blood and can damage the kidneys. It is a medical emergency.
> Get urgent medical care if severe muscle pain comes with:
> **Dark, cola or tea-coloured urine.**
> Marked swelling of the muscles.
> Weakness far beyond normal soreness, or being unable to use the limb.
> Passing little urine, nausea, confusion or fever.
> This does not mean you should worry about ordinary soreness. It means knowing the one picture that needs immediate attention.

## The basics
- **Sleep:** 7 or more hours. {aasm2015}
- **Enough food and protein.**
- **Gradual progress.**
- **Fluid across the day.**
- **Ordinary movement.** It is fine for its own sake.

## Common mistakes
- **Using soreness as your score.** It mostly reflects novelty.
- **Chasing it.** That adds recovery cost.
- **Assuming less pain means faster recovery.** The evidence is about soreness.
- **Stopping all movement.** Reducing is reasonable. You rarely need to stop.
- **Testing sore muscles with heavy loads.** That is a poor day to find out.
- **Raising volume so quickly you are badly sore again and again.** Slow down.

## Key takeaways
- Soreness reflects unfamiliar work more than productive work, and it fades with repetition.
- Cooling, massage, contrast therapy, phototherapy and kinesiotaping showed effects on soreness.
- Evidence for stretching, exercise and electrical stimulation is weak.
- Most of the underlying reviews were low quality, so hold this loosely.
- Less pain is not proof of faster recovery or better results.
- Severe pain with dark urine is an emergency.

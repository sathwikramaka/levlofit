## What a push session trains
A push session trains the muscles that press weight away from you: chest, front and side shoulders, and triceps. They work together, which makes the grouping convenient. Nothing requires you to train them this way.
Your Levlofit plan builds your sessions for you. This chapter teaches the reasoning, so you can judge a session, swap an exercise sensibly, or build one for your own equipment.

## Think in slots
Think of a session as an ordered set of slots, each with a job. Two arguments support this, resting on different evidence.
**Order.** The ACSM found strength was better when an exercise was trained at the beginning of a session rather than the end. Exercise order did not affect muscle growth. So put what you most want to get stronger at first. {acsm2026}
**Coverage.** Different exercises can grow different parts of a muscle.
> evidence: Evidence: emerging
> In 30 trained people who trained each leg differently for 8 weeks, leg extensions favoured one part of the quadriceps and leg press favoured another. This is one lower-body study. Applying it to pressing muscles is reasoning, not a tested result. {burke2025}
| Slot | Job |
| Main press | The heaviest, most technical press, done first. |
| Second press | Another pressing angle, or a more supported press, adding work at lower technical cost. |
| Chest isolation | A single-joint movement for the chest. |
| Side shoulder | Lateral raises, because pressing gives side shoulders little direct work. |
| Triceps | Direct work, after presses have already tired them. |
This is a checklist for coverage, not a law.

## Choosing an exercise for a slot
A slot names a job, not an exercise. Apply these in order:
- **Equipment.** Start with what you actually have.
- **Joints and injury history.** Remove anything that irritates a known problem.
- **Skill.** If you have less experience, prefer a shorter learning curve for the same job.
- **Fatigue.** Free weights ask more of your stabilising muscles. That is easiest to afford early. Later, a machine often lets you keep working the target muscle for less overall fatigue.
> evidence: Evidence: insufficient on free weights and machines for growth
> The ACSM found strength was not affected by free weights versus machines, and not enough data to say about growth. It also concludes that many forms of resistance training work. {acsm2026}
So choose the version you can load progressively and do consistently.

## Swapping exercises
Two exercises can replace each other when both are the same movement pattern and load the same joint action.
A flat barbell bench press and an incline dumbbell press are both presses using the shoulder and elbow in the same way. A cable fly is not a substitute for either. It targets the same muscle, but the elbow stays largely fixed, so it belongs in the isolation slot.
Use this test on any exercise, including ones this Handbook does not name.

## Volume, effort and frequency
**Volume** is hard sets per muscle per week, with assisting sets counted as half. Every press also counts as triceps work. The ACSM found growth was enhanced by 10 or more sets per muscle group per week, and a separate review found diminishing returns as sets rise. Reach about 10 if you can, then adjust based on recovery. {acsm2026} {pelland2025}
**Effort** is how close you get to failure. Make the final repetitions truly hard. Failure is not required, and it has not been shown to give consistently better results. [[exercise-science|Exercise science]] sets out the evidence. {refalo2023} {robinson2024} {acsm2026}
**Frequency** is how often you train a muscle. For strength, the ACSM found 2 or more sessions a week helped, and could not determine an upper limit. With volume equal, frequency did not change growth. {acsm2026} {pelland2025}
There is **no established fixed recovery time** such as 48 hours. It varies with the person, the exercise and the work done. Use the signals in [[recovery|Recovery]].
These dials add up. Raising volume without thinking about effort and frequency is a common route to a plan you cannot recover from.

## Rest between sets
Nothing supports shorter rest as better. A small benefit appears past about 60 seconds, with no clear further gain past 90. The ACSM found no strength difference, and not enough data on growth. {schoenfeld2024} {acsm2026}
Rest until the target muscle, not your breathing, limits the next set. Heavy presses usually need longer than isolation work to perform well.

## How to progress
| Model | How it works | Best for |
| Double progression | Fill a repetition range at one load, then add load. | Most muscle-building work. Repetitions absorb good and bad days. |
| Load progression | Add load at a fixed, usually low, repetition target. | Strength work. The ACSM links loads of 80% of your one-repetition maximum or more with greater strength gains. {acsm2026} |
| Adjusting by feel | Change load or sets based on how today's sets went. | Weeks where your recovery varies a lot. |
Match the model to your goal and repetition range. Load affects strength, but growth was not affected by loads from 30% to 100% of your maximum in the ACSM review. Heavier is not required for size. {acsm2026}

## Warm-up
[[warmup-mobility|Warm-up and mobility]] covers the evidence. For a push session: a few minutes of light activity, shoulder and upper back movement, then gradually heavier warm-up sets of your main press. Warm-up sets matter most. If time is short, cut the general part.

## Across the week
Plan recovery by which muscles you trained and how hard, not by session names. A chest day and a shoulder day share shoulder and triceps work whatever they are called.

## Worked example
This is one complete example, not a prescription. It fills the slots above so you can check your own reasoning.
| Slot | Exercise | Sets | Repetitions | Rest |
| Main press | Incline dumbbell press | 4 | 6 to 8 | 2 to 3 min |
| Second press | Machine chest press | 3 | 8 to 10 | 2 min |
| Chest isolation | Cable high-to-low fly | 3 | 10 to 12 | 90 sec |
| Side shoulder | Dumbbell lateral raise | 3 | 12 to 15 | 60 to 90 sec |
| Side shoulder | Cable lateral raise | 3 | 12 to 15 | 60 to 90 sec |
| Triceps | Overhead cable triceps extension | 3 | 10 to 12 | 75 to 90 sec |
| Triceps | Rope pushdown | 3 | 12 to 15 | 60 to 90 sec |
Count it per muscle: chest gets 10 direct sets, side shoulders get 6, and triceps get 6 direct sets plus half of the 7 press sets, about 9.5. Chest reaches 10 in this one session. Rest figures are ranges because the evidence does not support exact numbers.

## Common mistakes
- **Skipping warm-up sets.** Your first working set becomes the warm-up.
- **Swinging on isolation work.** It moves the load off the muscle you meant to train.
- **Going past your repetition range.** A load that undercuts the range throws away the reason you chose it.
- **Shortening range to add load.** See [[progressive-overload|Progressive overload]].
- **Changing exercises so often no method gets a fair trial.** Progress needs a stable reference.
- **Treating shorter rest as progress.** It is a time choice.

## Key takeaways
- A push session is a set of slots with jobs, not a fixed list.
- Put what you most want to get stronger at first.
- Aim for about 10 hard sets per muscle per week. Returns shrink as you add more.
- Train hard. Failure is not required.
- Nothing supports shorter rest as better.
- Choose exercises for fatigue and ease of learning. Machines and free weights both work.

## Chapter overview
Athletic training here means power: moving your own body quickly and forcefully. It is usually trained early in a session, before anything that would tire you.

## Plyometrics
> evidence: Evidence: strong
> A meta-analysis of 56 studies on jump training found the largest gains in jump height came from programmes longer than 10 weeks, with more than 20 sessions, and with more than 50 jumps a session. Combining jump types beat using only one. Adding weight gave no extra benefit. {saez2009}
Those are amounts for maximising jump height, not a starting point. If you are new to jumping, start with far less, and build up.
It makes practical sense to do this work fresh, since the quality of each jump matters.

## A movement menu
| Movement | What it trains |
| Pogo hop, then box jump | Quick rebound, then maximum vertical power |
| Broad jump | Horizontal power |
| Lateral bound | Side-to-side power |
| Medicine ball slam | Whole-body power through your trunk |
| Sprint acceleration | Straight-line speed from a standing start |

## A sensible order
This order is practical reasoning. Learn to control a landing before you try to jump higher. Learn to jump before you ask your legs to do the same at speed. Then build acceleration, slowing down and changing direction.
> warn: Get coaching for advanced work
> Top-speed sprinting and advanced jumps, such as depth jumps and high-volume single-leg bounding, have real technical demands. A qualified coach who can watch you in person will catch problems sooner. Build up slowly, and stop if you feel pain in a joint or tendon.

## Key takeaways
- Larger jump gains came from programmes of over 10 weeks and over 20 sessions.
- Combine jump types. Added weight gave no extra benefit.
- Train power when you are fresh.
- Land well first, then jump, then add speed.

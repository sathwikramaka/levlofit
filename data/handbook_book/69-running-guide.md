## Chapter overview
In running, the hardest session is not always the most important one. A running week can be built mostly from comfortable running, with faster work added in small amounts once that base is secure.

## Three kinds of session
These are common practical formats.
| Session | What it is | Purpose |
| Easy | 30 to 50 minutes at a pace where you can talk | Your aerobic base |
| Threshold | Two to four controlled 8-minute efforts, with easy jogging between | Sustained, comfortably hard running |
| Intervals | Three to five hard efforts of about four minutes, with longer easy recovery | Top-end aerobic fitness, in small doses |
With three running days, keep most of them easy. Add harder work slowly, one session type at a time.

## Why easy running comes first
> evidence: Evidence: strong, for health
> WHO recommends adults do 150 to 300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity a week, or 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous activity, or a mix. {who2020}
A week of only hard running is hard to sustain at that volume. A week of mostly easy running is much easier to keep up.

## Adding distance
> evidence: Evidence: uncertain, from one exploratory study
> A one-year study followed 874 novice runners. Overall, injury rates did not differ significantly between those increasing weekly distance by under 10%, 10 to 30%, or over 30%. {nielsen2014}
> For distance-related injuries specifically, those increasing by more than 30% had a higher rate than those under 10% (hazard ratio 1.59), but the interval included no effect (0.96 to 2.66). The authors suggest novices may be well advised to stay under 30% a week. {nielsen2014}
So no percentage is proven safe, including the popular "10% rule". A sensible approach is to change one thing at a time: distance, number of sessions or intensity. Pain that continues or gets worse is a reason to stop and get it checked.
> warn: Stop when quality drops
> If a hard session has fallen well below your intended pace, you are training a slower pace while tired. End it early. Stop right away if you feel chest pain, dizziness or unusual breathlessness.

## Key takeaways
- Build mostly on easy running.
- Add harder sessions slowly.
- No distance increase is proven safe. Over 30% a week looks less wise for novices, on uncertain evidence.
- Change one thing at a time.

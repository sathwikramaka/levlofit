## Chapter overview
Body composition is the balance of muscle and fat in your body. It changes for two reasons: your training, and your energy intake. This chapter covers choosing a direction, setting a pace, estimating your starting point, and measuring the result well enough to know if it is working.

## Choosing a direction
| Goal | Energy position | What decides success |
| Fat loss | Sustained deficit | Enough protein and continued training, so you lose mostly fat. |
| Staying the same | About balanced | An underrated choice. Strength can still improve at a stable weight. |
| Muscle gain | Small surplus | Keep it small. In practice, a big surplus mostly adds fat, because muscle grows slowly. |
These are reasonable defaults, not research findings:
| If you | Reasonable default |
| Are new to resistance training | Train and eat enough protein at about maintenance. Gaining muscle and losing fat together is most likely here. |
| Carry noticeable extra fat | Lose fat first. The ISSN says a larger deficit is more suitable at higher body fat. {aragon2017} |
| Are lean and want more muscle | A small surplus. |
| Are unsure | Maintenance, with attention to protein and training. It gives you time to decide. |

## Estimating your starting point
> warn: Every calorie estimate is a guess with a range
> Calculators give one confident number for your daily needs. Levlofit starts from an equation too. Individuals vary around any such estimate, and activity levels add more uncertainty. Treat the number as a starting guess, not a measurement.
Your own trend is more reliable. It takes two to three weeks:
- Eat in a fairly consistent way and log it.
- Weigh yourself in the same conditions most days: after waking, after using the toilet, before eating.
- Compare one week's average with the next. A stable average means you are near maintenance, whatever the calculator said.
- Adjust from there.

## Measuring: the seven-day average
Daily body weight can swing by a kilogram or more from water, food in your gut, salt, carbohydrate and, for many women, the menstrual cycle. These swings are often larger than a week's real change, so one weigh-in tells you little.
> tip: The rule to use
> **Judge your weight by a seven-day average, not a single reading.** Average the last seven mornings and compare that week to week.
Weight also cannot separate muscle from fat. Track it along with your lifts, measurements, photos in consistent conditions, and how your clothes fit.

## Pace of change
Use a percentage of your body weight, because the same kilograms mean very different things at different sizes.
| Goal | Pace | How firm this is |
| Fat loss | About 0.5 to 1% of body weight a week, slower as you get leaner | Moderate. One review found this pace may be better for keeping lean mass in lean, resistance-trained people. {helms2014} |
| Muscle gain | Slow enough that weight rises over months, not weeks | No precise figure is established. |
> evidence: Evidence: moderate
> Slower weight loss can better preserve lean mass in leaner people, and a larger deficit may suit those with higher body fat. Long-term success depends on sticking with it, which is another reason to avoid extreme deficits. Research in women and older people is limited. {aragon2017}

## Gaining muscle and losing fat together
This is often called recomposition. It is real, and practical experience suggests it is more likely in certain situations:
- You are new to resistance training.
- You are returning after a long break.
- You carry more body fat.
- Your protein is adequate and your training is consistent. These are requirements, not boosters.
No reliable rate is established. As you train for longer, it usually gets slower and harder, and working on one goal at a time may become simpler.

## You cannot choose where fat comes off
> warn: Spot reduction
> Training one body part is a poor way to lose fat from that area. Ab work builds your abs, but expect the fat over them to change as your overall body fat changes. Where you lose fat first is largely outside your control.

## Adjusting
Use these practical responses after two to three weeks of data:
| What you see | Reasonable response |
| Average flat, goal is fat loss | Eat a little less or move a little more. Change one thing. |
| Average falling faster than you intended | Add some food back. Faster is not better. |
| Average climbing quickly while gaining | Reduce the surplus. |
| Performance dropping across several exercises | Check sleep and total food before changing your plan. |
| Energy, mood or appetite staying low | Your deficit may be too large or have run too long. |
Adjust on trends, and change one thing at a time. Changing every week is usually too often to tell what worked.

## When this stops being a food question
> warn: Low energy availability and disordered eating
> Long or severe calorie restriction carries risks beyond your training plan. See a qualified clinician if you have:
> Missed or irregular periods.
> Frequent illness, lasting fatigue or repeated bone injuries.
> Low sex drive, disturbed sleep or often feeling cold.
> Distress about food or weight, restriction that feels hard to control, or eating that affects your daily life.
> These are medical concerns. Asking for help early is easier than asking later.

## Common mistakes
- **Treating a calculator as a measurement.** It is a starting guess.
- **Judging progress on one weigh-in.** Daily swings often exceed weekly change.
- **Cutting too hard.** It can cost muscle, training quality and your ability to keep going.
- **Bulking hard.** Much of the excess is likely to be fat.
- **Adjusting every few days.** That is too soon to separate signal from noise.
- **Letting protein drop in a deficit.** It matters most when food is lowest.

## Key takeaways
- Pick a direction and hold it long enough to judge. Maintenance is a fair choice.
- Calorie calculators are guesses. Your two-week trend is the measurement.
- Judge weight by a seven-day average.
- For fat loss, about 0.5 to 1% of body weight a week is a reasonable pace.
- Gaining muscle while losing fat can happen, with no reliable rate.
- You cannot choose where you lose fat.

## Chapter overview
Watches and machines give numbers for everything. Some are useful, some mislead. This chapter shows which to trust and how to use them.

## Your heart-rate zones
A simple estimate of your maximum heart rate is 208 minus 0.7 times your age. {tanaka2001} It is an estimate: your real maximum can be 10 beats or more either way.
:: band Intensity by % of maximum heart rate | % of max | {garber2011}
50 | 57 | Very light: easy walking | good
57 | 64 | Light: warm-ups, brisk walking | good
64 | 77 | Moderate: you can talk in sentences | good
77 | 96 | Vigorous: a few words at a time | ok
96 | 100 | Near maximum: short intervals only | bad
::
:: stats
208 - 0.7 x age | bpm | estimated maximum heart rate | {tanaka2001}
187 | bpm | estimated maximum for a 30-year-old | {tanaka2001}
120 to 142 | bpm | moderate zone (64 to 76%) for that 30-year-old | {garber2011}
::
Heart rate is most useful for steady cardio such as easy runs and cycling. During lifting and short intervals, wrist monitors lag and jump, so go by effort instead. Heat, caffeine, stress, poor sleep and illness all raise heart rate on a given day. See [[cardio-intensity|Easy, moderate and hard cardio]].

## The "fat-burning zone"
:: compare What it really means | What people think it means
At easy effort, a higher share of the energy you use comes from fat. | Easy cardio burns more fat in total.
Harder effort burns more energy per minute, so often as much fat or more in the same time. | You must stay in the zone to lose fat.
Easy effort lets you go longer and recover faster. | Hard cardio is wasted for fat loss.
::
Fat loss depends on your energy balance over days and weeks, not on the zone of one session. Choose the cardio you can do regularly. See [[calories-body-composition|Calories and body composition]].

## Sweat is not a score
Sweat cools you down. How much you sweat depends on heat, humidity, your genes, body size and fitness; fitter people often start sweating sooner. A dry session can be a great one, and a dripping one can be easy. To manage heavy sweating: train in cooler hours, wear light clothes, carry a towel, and replace fluid and salt on long or hot days. See [[hydration|Hydration and electrolytes]].

## How far to trust calories burned
:: bars Error in energy burned, 7 wrist devices tested | % average error | {shcherbina2017}
Most accurate device | 27 | still off by about a quarter
Least accurate device | 93 | nearly double or near zero
::
In a test of seven popular wrist devices, heart rate was fairly accurate but none measured energy burned well; even the best was off by about a quarter. {shcherbina2017} Gym machines and class estimates use averages that ignore your size and fitness, so they are no better. Treat calories-burned numbers as rough, and never "eat back" all of them. Judge your intake by the trend in your body weight instead.

## Cardio and lifting together
:: steps Mixing the two well
Cardio does not kill gains | Adding cardio to lifting does not reduce muscle growth overall; very large amounts of running may slightly reduce leg strength gains. {schumann2022}
Do the priority first | Whatever you most want to improve goes first in the session, while you are fresh. {murlasits2018}
Separate hard sessions | Hard intervals and heavy leg days are best on different days, or 6 hours or more apart.
Eat for both | Cardio uses energy; if you are trying to gain, eat a little more to cover it.
::
For general health it does not matter much which comes first; for strength goals, lift first. {murlasits2018} See [[combining-training|Combining different kinds of training]].

## Key takeaways
- Use 208 minus 0.7 times your age as a starting estimate of maximum heart rate.
- The fat-burning zone is about the share of fat, not the total; any cardio you keep up helps.
- Sweat cools you; it does not measure effort.
- Calories-burned numbers are rough; trust your weight trend.
- Lift first when strength is the goal; cardio helps, it does not kill gains.

## The problem this Handbook solves
Fitness does not lack information. The information is unsorted. A careful review of many trials and a confident video reach you through the same feed, in the same format, with the same air of authority. Nothing tells you which claim rests on twenty trials and which rests on an explanation someone found plausible.
The result is rarely that people fail to train. They train for years while changing what they do, and never build enough uninterrupted progress in one direction to see a result. What is usually missing is not effort. It is a stable framework, and a way to tell strong claims from weak ones.
This Handbook aims to tell you what to do, and also how confident anyone should be that it works.

## What this Handbook is
It is a reference covering training, nutrition, recovery, physique and tracking progress. Your Levlofit plan tells you what to do today. The Handbook explains why.
| Property | What it means for you |
| It explains | Recommendations come with their reasoning, so you can adapt them when your situation differs. |
| It grades its confidence | Where the evidence is weak or divided, it says so instead of choosing a side to read better. |
| It is made to be re-read | What is useful to you changes as you train for longer. |

## How evidence is graded
Claims carry one of six grades. These are the Handbook's own reading of the sources, not grades given by the papers.
| Grade | What it means | Wording used |
| Strong | Several good reviews, or a current position stand from a recognised body. | "Evidence shows" |
| Moderate | Consistent findings with real limits. | "Evidence suggests" |
| Emerging | Early human evidence. | "Early evidence indicates" |
| Insufficient | No adequate human evidence either way. | "Not established" |
| Contested | Credible evidence on both sides. | "Evidence is divided" |
| Practical | Sensible guidance, or personal preference, that research has not settled. | "In practice" |
Two rules follow. An explanation of how something might work inside the body is not a result, and does not justify a recommendation by itself. And "not established" means the question is open, not that the answer is no.
> tip: A useful habit
> When you meet a confident fitness claim, do not ask only whether it is true. Ask what someone would have had to measure to know it. Many popular claims fail that question straight away.

## Five working principles
These are working defaults, not scientific findings.
- **Consistency beats intensity.** The plan you follow for two years beats the better plan you follow for six weeks.
- **Technique defines the load.** A repetition done differently is a different exercise, whatever the log says.
- **Recovery is part of training.** It is an input to adaptation, not a reward for having trained.
- **Progress is measured in months.** Change from week to week is mostly noise: water, sleep, food timing and measuring error.
- **Evidence first, then what you will keep doing.** Where the evidence is absent, choose the option you will actually sustain.

## What progress looks like
One number makes a poor target. Body weight cannot separate muscle gained from food eaten, and a mirror cannot separate real change from better lighting. Track several things, because they move at different speeds and rarely all stall at once.
| What to track | What good looks like | Rough timescale |
| Strength | More work at the same standard of technique, on the same exercises. | Weeks |
| Physique | Balanced development, with body fat controlled rather than as low as possible. | Months |
| Health | Fitness, joint comfort and everyday movement improving or holding. | Months |
| Recovery | Handling more training than a year ago without building up fatigue. | Months to years |
| Sustainability | Still training, still enjoying it, still free of injury, years later. | Years |
The last row decides the others.

## How to use this Handbook
- **Read this volume in order.** The next few chapters give you the words every later chapter uses.
- **Use the rest as reference.** Go to the chapter that answers the question you have now.
- **Check the grade before acting.** A moderate claim deserves a trial in your training. An insufficient one deserves curiosity, not a change of plan.
- **Change one thing at a time.** Two changes together give a result you cannot explain.
- **Re-read the basics.** They become more useful as you train for longer.

## Scope and limits
> warn: What this Handbook is not
> This is general information for healthy adults. It does not diagnose conditions, read medical or lab results, or give individual medical, psychological or clinical nutrition advice.
> See a qualified health professional before starting or continuing if you have a diagnosed condition, are pregnant or have recently given birth, are managing an injury, have pain during exercise, or have a history of disordered eating. Nothing here should delay care you would otherwise seek.
Much of the research behind this Handbook studied young adults, more often men, over weeks to months. The further you are from that description, the more carefully you should apply it. Food examples are written for Indian kitchens and budgets. That shapes the examples, not the science.

## Key takeaways
- The problem is unsorted information, not missing information.
- Claims carry a visible grade. Check it before acting.
- An explanation is not a result, and "not established" does not mean false.
- Progress has several parts and is measured in months. Sustainability decides the rest.
- This is education, not clinical care.

## Chapter overview
The same questions come up for almost everyone who starts training. Here are short, honest answers, with the reasons.

## Toning, spot reduction and abs
:: compare Myth | Fact
I can tone a body part with light weights and high reps | "Toned" is some muscle plus low enough body fat to see it: build the muscle, lose the fat
Crunches burn belly fat; arm work burns arm fat | Training one area does not burn fat there: six weeks of ab exercises did not reduce belly fat. {vispute2011}
Some exercises target the lower belly | Where you lose fat first and last is set by your genes; often the first place you gain is the last to go
Abs need daily ab training | Abs show when body fat is low enough; train them two or three times a week like any muscle
::

## Will I get too big?
No. Building a lot of muscle takes years of hard training and eating a surplus on purpose; nobody does it by accident. If you ever feel bigger than you want, stop adding weight and sets, and you will stay where you are.

## Can I skip leg day?
Only for an injury, a doctor's restriction, or no access to the training. Running, cycling and sports are not a substitute for strength training: your legs and hips are your biggest muscles, and strong legs help those sports and protect your knees. See [[leg-sessions|Building a leg session]].

## Am I overtraining?
Almost certainly not. True overtraining takes months of far too much work with too little recovery and is rare. Feeling flat for a week is short-term fatigue: sleep more, eat enough, and take a lighter week if it lasts. See [[deload-week|Deload week]].
:: flow Tired and weaker for a while?
Weaker for 2 or more weeks despite good sleep and food? | Take a lighter week: half the sets, same weights. | It is a normal bad day or week: carry on.
Still weaker after the lighter week? | Check sleep, stress, illness and calories; see a doctor if you also feel unwell. | Return to your plan.
::

## Will lifting make me stiff?
Strength training through a full range of motion improves flexibility about as much as stretching does. {afonso2021} Stiffness comes from training only partial ranges or far more pushing than pulling. See [[mobility-flexibility|Mobility and flexibility]].

## Does lifting stunt growth?
No. Supervised strength training with good technique is safe for children and teenagers and builds strength, bone and confidence. {faigenbaum2009} Injuries in young lifters mostly come from poor supervision and badly chosen loads, not lifting itself. Levlofit's plans are for adults 18 and over.

## Exercise and mood
Exercise reduces symptoms of depression, with walking, jogging, yoga and strength training among the most effective options, and more vigorous exercise doing more. {noetel2024} It works alongside treatment, not instead of it. If low mood lasts for weeks or you have thoughts of harming yourself, talk to a doctor or call Tele-MANAS at 14416. On hard days, a short walk still counts.

## Soreness
Soreness means you did something new, not that you trained well. It fades as your body adapts, so no soreness after a few weeks is normal. You can train a sore muscle after a good warm-up; it often feels better once you move. See [[muscle-soreness|Muscle soreness]].

## Protein timing and shakes
:: compare Myth | Fact
I must have a shake within 30 minutes of training | Total daily protein matters most; timing adds little when daily protein is enough. {schoenfeld2013t}
The body can only use 30 g of protein at a time | A larger serving keeps working for longer; you do not waste protein above 30 g. {trommelen2023}
Protein shakes and mass gainers are special | They are powdered food: convenient protein or calories, nothing more
::
If you train on an empty stomach, eat a protein meal soon after. Otherwise eat protein spread over three or four meals and reach your daily target. See [[nutrient-timing|Meal timing]].

## Fitting in enough protein
Protein has 4 kcal per gram, so even 150 g a day is only 600 kcal. Build each meal around a protein food (dal, paneer, curd, eggs, chicken, fish, soya chunks, chana) and use a shake if you fall short. See [[protein|Protein]] and [[veg-protein|Vegetarian and vegan protein]].

## Supplements in one line each
Creatine monohydrate (3 to 5 g a day) has strong evidence; caffeine helps performance; vitamin D, B12 and omega-3 are worth it only to fix a low intake or a measured deficiency. Most other products are mostly marketing. Levlofit's Supplement guide covers timing and safe amounts. See [[supplements-guide|Supplements]].

## Key takeaways
- You cannot spot-reduce fat; "toned" is muscle plus low fat.
- You will not get big by accident; legs are not optional.
- Overtraining is rare; flat weeks need sleep, food and maybe a lighter week.
- Full-range lifting improves flexibility; supervised lifting is safe for young people.
- Total daily protein matters more than timing or serving size.

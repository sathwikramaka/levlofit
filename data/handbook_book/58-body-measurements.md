## Chapter overview
A tape measure is cheap and repeatable, and it directly measures something real. It also carries more error than a neat number suggests, and everything in this chapter follows from that.

## How accurate is self-measuring?
> evidence: Evidence: moderate, from one small study
> In 41 mothers of young children, self-measured waist, hip and neck measurements were compared with a trained technician's. Agreement was high (0.97, 0.96 and 0.84). Mean differences were small (-0.13 to 0.06 inches), and at least 93% of differences fell within the study's limits. {barrios2016}
> For waist, 93% of home measurements were within 10% of the technician's. {barrios2016}
> Duplicate measurements differed by 0.08 to 0.76 inches. {barrios2016}
So self-measuring can work well with simple instruction. But 10% of a 90 cm waist is 9 cm, far more than you would expect to change in a month. And this was a small group of women, so it may not fit everyone.

## What that means in practice
| Consequence | Why |
| Compare only with your own past measurements, taken the same way | Your own method is the usable signal. |
| Do not compare your number with someone else's | Different tape, spot and technique. |
| Treat a small change as no change | Duplicates alone varied by up to 0.76 inches, nearly 2 cm. {barrios2016} |

## Measure fewer sites
Eight noisy numbers a month will almost always show something moving. Try two, with a third if you like.
| Site | Status |
| Waist | Keep this one. It is your practical alternative to a body-fat reading. |
| Chest or upper arm | Reasonable when gaining muscle. Pick one. |
| Thigh | Optional. |
| Others | Measure if you are curious. Leave them out of your monthly review. |

## Technique
These do not make you match a technician. They make you match your own last measurement, which is what matters.
- **Same spot, written down.** Describe it once and follow it every time.
- **Same tape.** Fabric tapes can stretch.
- **Same time of day, before eating.** Waist size can vary with posture, time of day and whether you have eaten. {barrios2016}
- **Relaxed, breathing normally.** Do not hold your breath or suck in.
- **Tape flat on your skin, not pressing into it.**
- **Same measurer.**
- **Take three readings and record the middle one.**
> tip: If you menstruate
> The study's authors point to cycle phase as a possible cause of changes in abdominal size. {barrios2016} Measuring at the same point in your cycle removes one source of variation.

## How often
These intervals are practical guidance. No study here sets the best one.
| Interval | Verdict |
| Weekly or more | Not recommended. Expected change is smaller than the error. |
| Every four weeks | A sensible default. |
| Every three months | Better when gaining muscle, since change is slower. |

## Reading changes honestly
| What you see | What it supports |
| Waist down several centimetres over months, lifts holding | Fits fat loss with muscle kept. This takes months. |
| Arm or chest up 1 cm in a month | Nothing yet. Wait. |
| Everything up together | Unclear. Use your weight trend and log. |
| Nothing changed in three months | Worth looking into, but check your log first. |
| One reading far from the trend | Probably technique. Measure again. |
> warn: Do not act on one reading
> One tape measurement does not justify a decision that day. If a measurement decides your mood, whether you eat or whether you train, stop measuring and see a clinician.

## Common mistakes
- **Reporting more precision than a tape can give.**
- **Comparing your number with a published standard or another person.**
- **Measuring eight sites and treating the noisiest as a trend.**
- **Taking one reading instead of three.**
- **Changing your tape, spot, time or measurer.**
- **Sucking in, or measuring after eating.**
- **Acting on a 1 cm change.**

## Key takeaways
- Self-measuring can be fairly accurate, but small changes still sit inside the error.
- Compare only with your own past measurements, taken the same way.
- Measure two sites, take three readings, and record the middle one.
- Every four weeks at most, or three months when gaining.
- One centimetre is not a finding.

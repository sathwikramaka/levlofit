# Handbook v4: reviewer package

Status: local draft for review. Production handbook (v3 migration, builder, evidence register, passages file) is unchanged.
Nothing in this package is clinically approved; "verified" in a status means only that a quoted passage from the cited
source was matched. Generated by `data/handbook_v4/review_package.py` from `data/handbook_v4/claims_register.csv`.

## How to review
1. Decide the policy questions below (section "Policy decisions"); each lists options and consequences. Provisional choices, if any, are in handbook_v4_review/DECISIONS.md and approve nothing.
2. Work through the four item files. Every open claim appears once, in its primary section, with all its flags:
   - [1_clinical_safety.md](handbook_v4_review/1_clinical_safety.md): 120 claims, for a qualified clinician (and dietitian for nutrition).
   - [2_evidence_gaps.md](handbook_v4_review/2_evidence_gaps.md): 104 claims, for an evidence reviewer with library access.
   - [3_policy_decisions.md](handbook_v4_review/3_policy_decisions.md): 68 claims governed by a decision below.
   - [4_editorial.md](handbook_v4_review/4_editorial.md): 143 claims, for an editor or subject expert.
3. Or use the worksheet [review_items.csv](handbook_v4_review/review_items.csv) (same 435 items; blank columns for
   decision, new wording, source, reviewer and date). Return it; decisions are then applied to the draft and register.
Primary section order: policy decision, then clinical safety, then evidence gap, then editorial.

## Policy decisions (options; provisional choices are recorded in handbook_v4_review/DECISIONS.md and approve nothing)

### BMI cut-offs for Indian adults
Where it applies: body-measures, under-eating, calories/fat-loss rules; app: plan-engine.ts (underweight below 18.0, bands), tools.dart bmiBandIndia. PROVISIONAL choice recorded: option B, pending clinical review (docs/handbook_v4_review/DECISIONS.md); nothing applied yet.

- **A. Keep Misra 2009 consensus: underweight <18, normal 18-22.9, overweight 23-24.9, obese >=25**  
  Consequence: No code change. The cited paper has not been read (source_access), so the bands stay unverified. People with BMI 18.0-18.4 are treated as normal and can be offered a fat-loss phase.
- **B. Adopt ICMR Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024: normal 18.5-23, overweight 23-27.5, obese >27.5 (passage recorded, PDF pp. 81-82)**  
  Consequence: Handbook bands, plan-engine.ts thresholds, tools.dart and their tests change together. Fewer people are labelled obese; BMI 18.0-18.4 becomes underweight, so no fat-loss phase. Consistent with a current Indian national source.
- **C. Show both with an explanation, and choose one for app decisions**  
  Consequence: Most transparent; longer chapter; the app still needs one threshold, so A or B is still required for the engine.

Question for the decision-maker: Which cut-offs should the handbook and the app use, and is a clinician comfortable with the underweight threshold that blocks dieting?

### Running versus strength (interference)
Where it applies: one-step-at-a-time (why one goal first), hyrox, key points; the app's goal gating does not use this claim directly. PROVISIONAL choice recorded: option C, pending evidence review (docs/handbook_v4_review/DECISIONS.md); the draft now uses its cautious wording and an 'unverified' evidence grade.

- **A. Keep: frequent, long endurance work costs some strength, size and especially power (cites Wilson 2012, not read)**  
  Consequence: Current wording stays; unverified until Wilson 2012 is read.
- **B. Remove or substantially soften the interference claims. ACSM 2026 (read) reports insufficient data to determine whether aerobic and resistance training in the same session affects strength, muscle growth or power; it does not report 'no effect'**  
  Consequence: Removes the stronger claim; 'One goal at a time' then rests on learning and recovery rather than interference; the HYROX scheduling advice needs another reason or none.
- **C. Keep but label as uncertain: 'may reduce power and size gains, especially with long or frequent endurance sessions; evidence is mixed'**  
  Consequence: Cautious middle ground; still needs a reviewer to accept the wording.

Question for the decision-maker: Which wording best reflects the evidence, and does the One goal at a time rationale still hold?

### Vegetarian and vegan muscle-building
Where it applies: veg-protein (opening line, vegan staples), key points.

- **A. Keep: 'You can build muscle on a vegetarian or vegan diet. It takes planning.' (partly supported: ICMR 2024 on complete amino acids from combined plant foods)**  
  Consequence: Encouraging for a mostly vegetarian audience; the muscle-building part is not directly shown by a read source.
- **B. Soften: 'Vegetarian and vegan diets can support muscle gain when protein is planned; direct evidence in vegans is limited.'**  
  Consequence: More cautious; acknowledges ISSN 2017's cited trial in older men where fat-free mass rose on a meat diet and fell on a lacto-ovo-vegetarian one.
- **C. Find and cite a direct source first (for example a trial or review of plant versus animal protein and muscle gain), then decide**  
  Consequence: Needs a new source review before release.

Question for the decision-maker: Which wording should the handbook use, and is further evidence required first?

### Caffeine 630 mg example
Where it applies: supplements (caffeine bar chart: 3, 6 and 9 mg per kg for a 70 kg person).

- **A. Keep the 9 mg/kg = 630 mg bar labelled 'more side effects'**  
  Consequence: Shows the upper end honestly as a warning; a single dose above commonly cited daily limits may still read as a target to some users.
- **B. Remove the 630 mg bar; show only 3 and 6 mg/kg**  
  Consequence: Simpler and safer to read; loses the side-effect comparison.
- **C. Keep or remove the bar, and add a caution: daily caffeine limits, pregnancy, high blood pressure, sleep**  
  Consequence: Safest wording, but the limits need a source (for example EFSA 2015 on caffeine, not yet read) and clinical review.

Question for the decision-maker: Should the 630 mg example stay, and what caution (with which source) should accompany caffeine advice?

### Muscle-training frequency wording (change made in the draft; needs confirmation)
- Old wording (v3, build-muscle "Grows muscle" list): "Training each muscle twice a week". It was marked verified against
  ACSM 2026's general recommendation: "Our primary recommendation is that healthy adults perform RT with high effort ... at least twice weekly, with all major muscle groups being engaged".
- Conflict: Sets, reps and progress says frequency is "Any, once weekly sets are reached" for muscle size.
- New draft wording (`build-muscle:b04:do.items[2]`): "Reaching your weekly sets, split across the week however suits you".
- Evidence (ACSM 2026, PMC12965823 (article body)): "higher volume (≥10 sets/muscle group/wk) [...] RT did not influence hypertrophy performed with low (1 d/wk) versus high (>5 d/wk) frequency when total volume was equated".
- Twice-weekly training stays in How training works and Sets, reps and progress as ACSM's general advice.
- Question: approve the new wording, amend it, or restore the old one with a different source?

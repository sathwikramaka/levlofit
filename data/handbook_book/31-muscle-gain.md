title: Gaining muscle and weight
summary: How big a surplus to eat, how fast to gain, and why more food is not more muscle.

## Chapter overview
Muscle is built slowly. A small surplus plus hard training adds muscle; a big surplus mostly adds fat.

## How fast to gain
For beginners and intermediates, as a share of body weight each week: {iraki2019}
| Weekly gain | What it means |
| Up to 0.25% | Slow and lean |
| 0.25% to 0.5% | Target pace |
| 0.5% to 1% | Mostly fat |
Aim for a surplus of 10 to 20% above maintenance. Levlofit starts at 10%. {iraki2019}

## How much protein
> evidence: Evidence: strong
> In a meta-analysis of 49 trials, protein beyond about 1.6 g per kg a day gave no further average gain in muscle from training. {morton2018}
See [[protein|Protein]] for the full picture.

## Eating more when you are never hungry
- **Add a meal.** Milk and a banana, or curd and poha, between meals.
- **Use dense foods.** Peanuts, a little ghee on roti, paneer, dry fruits.
- **Drink some calories.** Milk, lassi or a banana shake.
- **Weigh weekly.** Levlofit rule: if the trend is off pace for 3 weeks, adjust by 150 to 200 kcal a day.

## Lean gain or dirty bulk
| Lean gain | Dirty bulk |
| Small surplus, weight up slowly | Eating everything in sight |
| Hard training 3 to 5 days a week | Gaining over 1% a week |
| Advanced lifters: aim for the lower end | Long fat-loss diet needed afterwards |

## Key takeaways
- A small surplus, about 10% above maintenance, adds muscle with less fat.
- Protein beyond about 1.6 g per kg gave no extra average muscle gain.
- A big surplus mostly adds fat.

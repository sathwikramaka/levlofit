## Chapter overview
The Nutrition volume covered how energy balance works. This chapter is about running a deficit for months: what protects your muscle, how far to trust a body-fat reading, and when getting leaner stops being a physique project and becomes a health problem.

## The pace of loss
About 0.5 to 1% of body weight a week is a reasonable pace, at the lower end as you get leaner. {helms2014} Slower loss can better preserve lean mass in leaner people, while a larger deficit may suit those with more body fat. {aragon2017}

## What protects muscle in a deficit
| What to do | What the ISSN says |
| Keep lifting | Resistance training has shown an impressive ability to preserve muscle during very low-calorie dieting, at least in untrained or obese people. {aragon2017} |
| Raise protein | 2.3 to 3.1 g per kg of **fat-free** mass may be required in lean, resistance-trained people eating below their needs. {aragon2017} |
Extra cardio, fat burners and meal timing are smaller than these two.
> tip: What decides the outcome
> The ISSN states that the long-term success of a diet depends on sticking with it. {aragon2017} A diet you finish beats a stricter one you drop in week six.

## You cannot choose where it comes off
Training one body part is a poor way to lose the fat over it. Expect that fat to change as your overall body fat changes. Later chapters in this volume are about building what is underneath, which is a separate job.

## Body-fat percentage is a weaker number than it looks
> evidence: Evidence: moderate, from one study
> Three body-fat scanners using electrical impedance were compared with a DEXA scan in 93 people. Agreement was unacceptable for a consumer scale (0.61), poor for a gym analyser (0.88) and moderate for a medical analyser (0.91). {merrigan2022}
> When 36 people retested after 12 weeks, the subtle changes in body fat did not agree between these devices and DEXA. {merrigan2022}
> All three repeated well within a session. Consistent is not the same as accurate. {merrigan2022}
So treat a body-fat reading as a wide band, not an exact figure. A scale reading of 17.4% is false precision. Your waist measurement, seven-day average weight, training log and photos are more practical. The body-fat tool in Levlofit gives an estimate too, so read it the same way.

## How body fat tends to look
This table is a rough convention for men, not a measurement. Women carry more body fat at a similar appearance, so these numbers do not transfer. Two people at the same reading can look very different.
| Approximate range (men) | Commonly described look |
| 20% and above | Little visible definition |
| Mid to high teens | Some shape, little abdominal definition |
| Low teens | Upper abs visible for many people |
| Around 10% | Clear abdominal definition |
| Below 10% | Very lean, and hard to hold year-round |

## When getting leaner becomes a health problem
> evidence: Evidence: consensus statement
> The International Olympic Committee describes Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport as a syndrome of harmful health and performance outcomes in female and male athletes exposed to low energy availability, meaning too little food for the exercise they do. {mountjoy2023}
> That statement is about athletes, so it is used here for its warning signs, not for numbers about you.
> warn: Stop cutting and see a clinician if
> Your periods become irregular or stop.
> You get a bone stress injury.
> Sex drive, mood, sleep or tolerance of cold fall together over weeks of dieting.
> You get ill more often, or small injuries stop healing as usual.
> Your strength keeps falling even though you are training as planned.
> Food rules keep narrowing, eating brings guilt or compensation, or the scale decides how your day goes.
> You cannot stop cutting, even after reaching your target.
> None of this is a failure of discipline. See a doctor, and where eating is involved, someone who works with disordered eating.
**A physique goal is not a good enough reason to keep up a deficit that is causing these signs.**

## Signs a cut is going well
| Sign | What it tells you |
| Seven-day average weight trending down | Your main weight signal. |
| Waist falling | Cheap and repeatable. |
| Logged loads roughly holding | Some drop is normal. A steady collapse is not. |
| Photos at fixed intervals | Slow, but honest. |
| You could keep eating like this | Sticking with it decides the outcome. {aragon2017} |

## Common mistakes
- **Reading a body-fat scale as a measurement.** It is a wide estimate.
- **Cutting faster to finish sooner.** Slower loss can protect lean mass if you are lean.
- **Dropping protein because calories are tight.** Protein matters most here.
- **Stopping resistance training during a cut.** It helps preserve your muscle.
- **Using endless cardio.** It costs recovery.
- **Training one area to lean it out.** Overall fat loss is what matters.
- **Pushing through warning signs because you have not hit your target.** Stop and get help.

## Key takeaways
- About 0.5 to 1% of body weight a week, slower as you get leaner.
- Resistance training and enough protein protect muscle in a deficit.
- Sticking with it decides the outcome.
- A body-fat reading is a band, and a poor way to track change.
- You cannot choose where you lose fat.
- There is a point where leaner is worse, and it has clear warning signs.

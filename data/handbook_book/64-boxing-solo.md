## Chapter overview
Boxing is two activities with one name. One is solo technique and conditioning: footwork, shadowboxing, bag work and skipping. The other is partner work, including sparring. This chapter covers only the first. This whole chapter is practical guidance.

## The boundary
> warn: Sparring and partner drills need a coach
> Do not spar from a handbook or an app. A qualified coach in the room can watch your guard, correct your distance and stop a session the moment it becomes unsafe. Levlofit cannot do that. Find a registered club and coach for any partner work, and get medical attention after any head injury.

## Solo training
| Movement | What it trains |
| Shadowboxing | Stance, guard, footwork and combinations |
| Heavy bag | The same combinations against real resistance, at a set pace |
| Skipping rope | Rhythm and conditioning |
| Footwork drills | Quick, precise steps without crossing your feet |
A classic format is rounds: three minutes of work and one minute of rest. Start with about three rounds and build up slowly. If your session is short, do fewer rounds instead of cutting a drill out of the rotation.
On the heavy bag, wrap your hands and wear gloves, and build up how hard you hit. Stop if your wrists, knuckles or shoulders hurt.

## Building a week
Keep some strength training in your week, with about two sessions, and fill the rest with solo rounds. Combined training generally does not hurt strength or muscle gains. {schumann2022}

## Key takeaways
- Solo boxing training is real training.
- Sparring and partner drills need a qualified coach in person.
- Rounds of three minutes on and one minute off are a classic format.
- Keep some strength training in your week.

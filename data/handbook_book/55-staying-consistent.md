summary: What is linked with still training in year three.

## Chapter overview
Many people stop training. What distinguishes those who do not? The evidence has a partial answer, and it does not come down to willpower.

## Your reasons
> evidence: Evidence: moderate
> A review of 66 studies found consistent support for a link between more autonomous motivation and exercise. Valuing the outcome tended to predict starting, while intrinsic motivation better predicted long-term exercise. Feeling competent also predicted exercise. Most studies were non-experimental, so this shows association, not proven cause. {teixeira2012}
In practice, the reason that gets you started may not be the one that keeps you going. It helps if training becomes something you would do anyway.

## How long before it takes less effort
Median time to form a habit was 59 to 66 days, and individuals ranged from 4 to 335 days. {smith2024} Someone at the far end took most of a year and was not doing anything wrong.

## Interruptions are normal
Stress predicted less physical activity in 76.4% of 55 prospective studies. Some found the opposite, with habitually active people exercising more under stress. {stults2014} So the years you have already put in may help protect you.
Missing one opportunity did not materially affect habit formation in one well-known study. {lally2010} "Never miss twice" is a catchy saying, not a finding. Keep it if it helps, but do not treat a second miss as failure.

## Your reduced week
When a month is hard, choose between your full plan and a smaller one, not your full plan and nothing. These are practical suggestions.
| What gets in the way | Reduced version |
| No time | Do fewer exercises. Cut volume on purpose instead of watering down every set. |
| Travelling | Do whatever is available. |
| Illness | Rest, and return at a lower load. |
| High stress | Protect attendance over intensity. The documented cost of stress is missed sessions. {stults2014} |
| Low mood for weeks, beyond training | This is not a training problem. See a clinician. |

## Common mistakes
- **Treating consistency as a character trait.** It also depends on your conditions.
- **Expecting training to feel automatic on a schedule.**
- **Reading one missed session as a broken streak.**
- **Having no reduced week ready.**
- **Building everything on a result.** Intrinsic motivation is more linked with continuing. {teixeira2012}
- **Changing your plan during a hard month.** Attendance dropped, not your plan's quality.
- **Treating weeks of flatness beyond training as a discipline problem.** See a clinician.

## Key takeaways
- Reasons that come from you are linked with continuing.
- A habit often takes about two months, and can take close to a year.
- Stress costs sessions, and an established habit may protect them.
- One missed session does not undo the process.
- Define your reduced week in advance.
- Persistence is not the answer to everything. Some signs need a clinician.
